ANALYSIS

Goal. Learn to disagree so the other person stays in the conversation, by acknowledging something real before you turn.

ONE QUESTION RUNS THIS WHOLE UNIT

Have I named something they actually said?

A general acknowledgment - I hear you - is heard as a formality. Naming their actual point is what makes the turn survivable.

THE FORM

Acknowledge-then-pivot connectorsI see where you're coming from, but ...; that said, ...
Adversative connectorshowever, ...; that is not to say ...It is real. However, we've mitigated most of it already. Watch out Don't let but erase the acknowledgment. “I hear you, but you're wrong” cancels the acknowledgment instead of building on it. Keep the acknowledgment specific and genuine: “I hear the concern about timeline, but the data points the other way.”

The acknowledgment has to be specific

I see where you're coming from works here because the next clause shows she understood what he said. Alone, it is a formula.

I hear the concern about timeline is worth more than I hear you, because it proves the listening rather than claiming it.

This is not politeness. A colleague who believes he was misunderstood will make the same point again, and the meeting runs twice.

The test you can run with no book

Before you say but, ask: could they tell from what I just said that I understood their point?

If your acknowledgment would fit any objection at all, it is not an acknowledgment. It is a throat-clear.

The mechanical half: everything after but, however or that said is your position, and everything before it is theirs. Whatever ends the sentence is what the room acts on.

Turning twice

Maria turns three times in four lines: but the data doesn't support waiting, then that said, I take the risk seriously, then however, we've mitigated most of it.

The middle turn is the one that matters. Without it she has won the argument and left him with nothing; with it, the conversation ends nearer him than a single but would allow.

The pattern is position, concession, position. It costs one extra sentence and it is the difference between agreement and compliance.

The three connectors are not the same register

But is neutral and spoken. However is written and formal, and in speech it slows the room down deliberately. That said and then again are spoken and signal a turn back toward the other person.

However after a full stop is a stronger break than but inside a sentence, which is why Maria uses it for the mitigation point and not for the first turn.

Mixing them up does not break the grammar; it breaks the pacing, and in a disagreement the pacing is most of the message.

Watch out

Don't let but erase the acknowledgment. “I hear you, but you're wrong” cancels the acknowledgment instead of building on it. Keep the acknowledgment specific and genuine: I hear the concern about timeline, but the data points the other way.

However takes a full stop or a semicolon before it. Say The risk is real. However, we've mitigated it. “The risk is real, however we've mitigated it” runs two sentences together.

The moves

Same pushback in every line. The highlighted slot changes; the acknowledgment always comes before the pivot.

Granting something real

  • I see where you're coming from, but the data doesn't support [waiting / a delay].
  • I hear the concern about [timeline / capacity], but the risk is on the other side.
  • That said, I take the [risk / concern] seriously.

Holding the position

  • The risk is real. However, we've [mitigated most of it / covered the main exposure].
  • Then again, [the window closes in March / the client has already scheduled around it].
  • Fair point, but it doesn't change [the recommendation / where we land].

A gentle acknowledgment sits on the left. A confident, evidence-backed pushback sits on the right.

Why this works

Disagreement that skips the acknowledgment is heard as dismissal, and a colleague who feels dismissed makes the same point again later, usually to someone else. The concessive move costs one clause and buys the attention your position needs. It also protects you: naming their point accurately forces you to have understood it, and half the disagreements in a working week dissolve at exactly that step.
