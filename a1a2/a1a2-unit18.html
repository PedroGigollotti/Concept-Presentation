ANALYSIS

Goal. Seven words, five pairs to learn, and one position. By the end you can stop repeating names and say what everybody else says.

ONE QUESTION RUNS THIS WHOLE UNIT

In front of the verb, or after it?

Ask it before you speak. In front of the verb you use unit 1's seven: I, you, he, she, it, we, they. After the verb you use these seven: me, you, him, her, it, us, them. Two of them are the same word in both places, so there are five pairs to learn.

THE FORM

I →meCan you send me her number?
he →himI can call him tonight.
she →herI like her.
we →usThey invite us to lunch.
they →themCan we invite them to lunch?
you and it →the same word in both placesI can text it to you now.

Seven words for after the verb

Unit 1 gave you the seven words that start a sentence. These are the seven that come after the verb, and they stand for the same seven.

Five of them change: I → me, he → him, she → her, we → us, they → them.

Two of them do not change at all. You stays you, and it stays it. That is five pairs to learn, not seven.

So learn them in pairs, out loud, in that order. Say the five pairs now, before you read on: ten words, and you have both halves of the system.

The position decides, and nothing else does

This is the whole rule, and it has no exceptions. In front of the verb, unit 1's word. After the verb, this unit's word.

Sam says “I like her” and Mia says “She needs help.” The same person, one line apart, in two positions.

It has nothing to do with who is more important or who is speaking. Count the words: is it before the verb, or after it?

So find the verb first, then choose. Never “I like she” and never “Her needs help”.

After to, with and for, the same word

These words also come after the short words that name a direction or a companion, and it is the same list.

Mia says “I can text it to you” and “help with the report.” After to, the after-the-verb word.

The same for with, for and about: come with me, this is for him, ask them about it.

So treat those short words the way you treat a verb here. What comes after them takes the same seven words.

Two of them in one sentence, and the order

Many sentences need both a person and a thing, and English puts them in a fixed order.

Mia says “Can you send me her number?” The person comes first and the thing second, with nothing between them.

You can also turn it round, and then to arrives: I can text it to you. The thing first, then to, then the person.

So pick one of the two orders and say the sentence that way every time. Both are in the scene, one line apart, and English allows no third.

Watch out

After the verb, never the word from unit 1. Say “I like her.” Never “I like she.” Find the verb, and choose by which side of it you are on.

In front of the verb, never the word from this unit. Say “She needs help.” Never “Her needs help.”

The person goes before the thing, or after to. Say “Send me the number.” or “Send it to me.” Never “Send to me it.”

The moves

Find the verb, then choose the side. The frame does not change; only the blue words do.

after the verb

  • I can [call, text] [her, him] tonight.
  • She [knows, needs] [us, them].
  • Can you [send, text] [me, us] the number?
  • We can [invite, call] [them, him].

after to, with and for

  • Send it [to me, to her].
  • Come [with us, with them].
  • This message is [for him, for you].
  • Ask [her, them] about [it, lunch].

The same person on the two sides of the verb, and the word changes with the side.

Why this works

Without these words you repeat every name in every sentence, and it marks you at once. With them you sound like everybody else in the room. There are only ten words in the whole system, two of which you already know, and the rule is a position rather than a meaning.

Over to you

Not graded. This is where the form stops being a rule and becomes a sentence you say.

Say the five pairs out loud, in order, twice. I and me, he and him, she and her, we and us, they and them.

Now say six sentences about the people at your desk, each with one of the seven after the verb.

Ask your partner for four things with Can you send me and Can you text it to me. Both orders, twice each.
