ANALYSIS
------------------------------------------------------------------------
   >>> INVENTED: The three cards are mine: the strong part, oh against zero, and the American compression. A different three -- ordinals, fractions, dates -- would make an equally defensible unit. The first card is also the only place in the book so far that teaches a SOUND rather than a form.

Goal. Hear the difference between “thirteen” and “thirty”. Then say a room, an extension, and a price.

THE STRUCTURE
Numbers are not new grammar. They are complements of “to be”. You already have that. The new thing is the SOUND. Three things change. One, where the strong part falls. Two, how big numbers are grouped. Three, when a number is one digit at a time.
   | thirteen / thirty  ·  Form: thir‑ TEEN · THIR ‑ty  ·  Meaning: In the teens the strong part is at the end. In the tens it is at the front.  ·  Use it to: hear the difference, and be heard  ·  example: Three, zero... Not one, three.
   | zero, or oh  ·  Form: four oh six · zero percent  ·  Meaning: In a room, an extension or a phone number, say “oh”. When it does not land, say “zero”: nothing else sounds like it.  ·  Use it to: give a number one digit at a time  ·  example: My extension is four oh six.
   | fifteen hundred  ·  Form: fifteen hundred = 1,500  ·  Meaning: In American English a big number is often two pairs. It is not thousands.  ·  Use it to: say a price or a quantity  ·  example: It is fifteen hundred.

WATCH OUT

   [ The strong part is the whole difference. “Thirteen” and “thirty” have the same sounds. Only the strong part changes. Make it longer, not louder. Say it again, one digit at a time. When someone hesitates, do not repeat the word. Say “Three, zero... Not one, three.” That always works. Percent has no plural. Say “fifteen percent,” never “× fifteen percents.” ]

   [ Short forms. A price drops the words for the money. “Nine ninety‑nine” is $9.99. “Fifteen fifty” is $15.50. At work the context tells you which one. ]

THE MOVES
Ready-made lines for a number. Change only the words in blue.
   - Is it room [thirty]?
   - My extension is [four oh six].
   - The price is [nine ninety-nine].
   - The discount is [fifteen] percent.
   - Sorry, is that [thirteen] or [thirty]?

WHERE THE STRONG PART FALLS
The same sounds. The strong part moves.
   (figure) The tall block is the strong part. In the teens it is at the end. In the tens it is at the front.

WHY THIS WORKS
Numbers are the trap for a good speaker. The grammar around them is nothing. But a wrong room costs you the meeting. A wrong price costs more than a wrong tense. Two of these are traps for you. “Thirteen” and “thirty” are one strong part apart. Neither Portuguese nor Italian uses stress that way. And an American says a big number in pairs.
