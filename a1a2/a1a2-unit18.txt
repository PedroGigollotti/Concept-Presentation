ANALYSIS
------------------------------------------------------------------------
Goal. Learn the three words for where. Then learn the same three for when.

THE STRUCTURE
Three words do this work. “In” is for the inside of something. “On” is for a surface. “At” is for a point. The same three do time. Say “in” a month, “on” a day, “at” a clock time. Big to small.
   | in  ·  Form: in + a space, or a long time  ·  Meaning: Inside it.  ·  Use it to: name a room, a car, a month  ·  example: in room four
   | on  ·  Form: on + a surface, or a day  ·  Meaning: Touching the top of it.  ·  Use it to: name a floor, a bus, a Monday  ·  example: on the second floor
   | at  ·  Form: at + a point, or a clock time  ·  Meaning: Right there, and no bigger.  ·  Use it to: name a desk, a door, nine o'clock  ·  example: at nine

WATCH OUT

   [ In a car, but on a bus. Say “in the car” and “on the bus.” A car is small and you sit inside it. A bus, a train and a plane take “on”. Nobody can explain it; everybody says it. In the morning, but at night. Say “in the morning,” “in the afternoon” and “in the evening.” Then say “at night.” Never “× in the night” for the ordinary meaning. Big to small. If you forget one, ask how big it is. A month is big: “in”. A day is one square on a calendar: “on”. A clock time is a point: “at”. ]

   [ Two that take no word at all. Say “today” and “tomorrow” on their own. Never say “× on today”. ]

FROM THE BIGGEST TO THE SMALLEST
Three words. They run from a big space down to one point.
   (figure) Read down a stop: the place, then the time. The word does not change when the job does.

WHY THIS WORKS
These three words are almost too small to hear. They carry the floor, the room, the day and the hour. Get one wrong and somebody corrects you. Miss them and the facts do not join up. Then you arrive on the wrong floor.
