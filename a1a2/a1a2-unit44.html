ANALYSIS

Goal. One noun, four words that can follow it, and the three sentences a Portuguese speaker gets wrong every time.

ONE QUESTION RUNS THIS WHOLE UNIT

Which noun am I saying more about?

Find the noun first, then put the second part straight after it. Use who for a person, which for a thing, that for either one, and where for a place.

THE FORM

a person →who or thatthe designer who joined last week
a thing →which or thata tool which tracks our tasks
a place →wherethe room where you meet
person or thing →thatthe one that has the big screen
the noun is the object →nothing at allthat app you use
never →the pronoun a second timethe app that I use it

Say the noun, then say which one

You already have two short sentences for this: “She's the designer. She joined last week.”

English joins them at the noun. Say the noun, then put the second sentence straight after it and start it with one small word.

That gives you Mia's line: “She's the designer who joined last week.” One sentence, one breath.

So find the noun first, and put the second part next to it. Nothing may come between them.

Who for a person, which for a thing, that for both

The choice is made by the noun in front, and there are only three words to choose from.

A person takes who: “the designer who joined last week.” A thing takes which: “a tool which tracks our tasks.”

That covers both, and it is what people say most: “the one that has the big screen.”

So look at the noun, then choose. Use that when you are not sure, and you will be right.

Where for a place

A place takes its own word, and it is the same where you already ask questions with in unit 11.

Leo says “the room where you meet”, and the second part says which room.

Say where only for a place. A person in that place still takes who, and a thing in it still takes which.

So name three places in your office now, and add a second part to each one with where.

The three sentences you will get wrong

One word does this whole job in your own language, so all three errors are the same habit.

Never “the app that I use it.” That is already the app. Do not say it twice.

Never “She's the designer joined last week.” The word is missing, and never “the tool what tracks” either.

So say the three right sentences out loud now: the app that I use, the designer who joined, the tool that tracks.

Watch out

Never say the noun twice. Say “the app that I use.” Never “the app that I use it.” The word at the front is already the app.

What is a question word, not a joining word. Say “the tool that tracks.” Never “the tool what tracks.”

Keep the second part next to its noun. Say “the room where you meet is upstairs.” Never “the room is upstairs where you meet.”

The moves

A noun, then the word, then the rest. The frame does not change; only the blue words do.

a person or a thing

  • She's the [colleague, developer] who [joined us, sits here].
  • It's a [tool, app] which [tracks, saves] our [tasks, work].
  • It's the [app, printer] that we [use, share].
  • That's the [button, one] that you [press, want].

a place

  • This is the [room, floor] where we [meet, work].
  • That's the [office, building] where she [works, started].

One noun, and the second part attached to it. The small word sits between them and never moves.

Why this works

Without this unit you speak in short separate sentences and sound like a list. With it you answer “which one?” inside the same sentence, which is what the rest of the book has been building towards.
