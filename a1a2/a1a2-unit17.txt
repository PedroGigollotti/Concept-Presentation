ANALYSIS
------------------------------------------------------------------------
Goal. Learn the second set of pronouns. Then learn where each set goes.

THE STRUCTURE
English has two sets. One does the action: “I”, “he”, “she”, “we”, “they”. One gets it: “me”, “him”, “her”, “us”, “them”. The second set goes AFTER the verb, and after “to” or “for”. It never comes first.
   | after the verb  ·  Form: verb + me / him / her / us / them  ·  Meaning: Who gets it.  ·  Use it to: say who the thing goes to  ·  example: She pays him.
   | after to and for  ·  Form: to / for + the second set  ·  Meaning: Where it ends up.  ·  Use it to: route a thing to a person  ·  example: Send it to me.
   | you and it never change  ·  Form: you and it, in both places  ·  Meaning: Two of the eight are free.  ·  Use it to: stop worrying about those two  ·  example: I call you. You call me.

WATCH OUT

   [ Not the first set, after a verb. Say “Call him.” Never say “× Call he.” This unit exists for that mistake. Not the first set, after to or for. Say “for us” and “to them.” Never say “× for we” or “× to they.” It goes after, not before. Say “I call her.” Never say “× I her call.” Your language can put it in front of the verb. English cannot. ]

   [ Two things and two people. The person can come first, or last with “to”. “Give the file to her” is correct. “Give her the file” is correct too. Never say “× Give the file her”. ]

THE TWO SETS, SIDE BY SIDE
One set does it. One set gets it. Only “you” is the same in both.
   (figure) The word on the right never starts a sentence. The word on the left never follows a verb.

WHY THIS WORKS
Everything at work goes TO somebody. A file, a message, a question. The pronoun is the address on it. It is one syllable long. Say “call he” and people understand you. Miss the “me” in “send it to me first”. Then the file goes out unchecked.
