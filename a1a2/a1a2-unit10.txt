ANALYSIS
------------------------------------------------------------------------
Goal. Learn the tense for what is always true. Then add the “-s” for he, she and it.

THE STRUCTURE
The present simple is for what is always true: habits and facts. For I, you, we and they, use the plain verb: “I work here”. For he, she and it, add “-s”: “she works here”. That small “-s” is the whole unit.
   | I, you, we, they  ·  Form: the plain verb  ·  Meaning: Always true.  ·  Use it to: say what you do every week  ·  example: We start at nine.
   | he, she, it  ·  Form: the verb + s  ·  Meaning: Always true, for one other person.  ·  Use it to: say what somebody else does  ·  example: She works here.
   | always, not now  ·  Form: the present simple, against “am / is / are”  ·  Meaning: “She works here” is her job. “She is here” is today.  ·  Use it to: say which one you mean  ·  example: She works here. She is not here today.

WATCH OUT

   [ Do not forget the -s. Say “She works,” never “× She work.” The “-s” changes no meaning. That is why it goes missing. A native ear hears the gap anyway. Only he, she and it take the -s. Say “They work,” never “× They works.” “I”, “you”, “we” and “they” use the plain verb. ]

   [ Watch the spelling. Most verbs add “-s”: “works”, “starts”. After o, ch, sh, s and x, add “-es”: “goes”, “watches”. After a consonant and y, y becomes “-ies”: “study”, “studies”. ]

ALWAYS, OR TODAY
   (figure) “She works here” is her job. “She is here” is a Tuesday.

WHY THIS WORKS
This tense is how you answer what do you do. It is the first thing a new colleague asks. It is the first thing you say about yourself. The “-s” carries no meaning at all. Your ear does not miss it. Your listener does. Say the verbs aloud with he and she. Let the “-s” become part of the word.
