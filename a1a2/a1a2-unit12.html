Adverbs of frequency

How often, and where the word sits.

ANALYSIS

Goal. Five words and one position. By the end you can say how often you do anything, and put the word where an English speaker puts it.

ONE QUESTION RUNS THIS WHOLE UNIT

Which verb is it, and where does the word go?

Look at the verb before you place the word. If the verb is be, the word goes after it: I'm always tired. If it is any other verb, the word goes in front of it: I sometimes play tennis. One question, two answers, and no third case.

THE FORM

all the time →alwaysI always meet friends.
most times →usuallyI usually go running.
many times →oftenI often feel tired too.
a few times →sometimesI sometimes play tennis.
no times →neverI never do sports.
with be →after the verbI'm always tired.

Five words, from all the time to none

Learn them as one line, in order, and you will never hunt for the middle one: always, usually, often, sometimes, never.

Sam uses four of them about himself in three lines, and Mia uses the fifth. That is how often you will need them.

They answer a question nobody has to ask out loud. Say how often, and the person you are talking to knows what your week looks like.

Say the five out loud in order twice now. The order is the meaning, and once you have the order you never confuse two of them.

In front of the verb, and that is not where your language puts it

This is the half to slow down for. Most languages put the word at the end of the sentence. English puts it inside.

Sam says “I sometimes play tennis” and “I usually go running.” The word sits between the person and the verb, and nothing separates them.

Never “I play tennis sometimes on the weekend.” as your normal way of saying it. The end of the sentence is where the time goes, not how often.

So build the sentence without the word first, then push the word in front of the verb. Do it that way ten times and the position stops needing thought.

With be, the word goes after

One verb takes the word on the other side, and it is the verb you use most.

Mia says “I'm always tired” and Sam says “I'm never bored.” The word comes after am, not in front of it.

Every other verb keeps the word in front: I never do sports. We usually watch a movie.

So ask which verb it is before you place the word. Never “I always am tired.”

Never is already the no

You have just spent a unit putting don't and doesn't into sentences. This word does that job by itself.

Mia says “I never do sports.” The verb after it is positive, and the sentence means she does none.

Never “I don't never do sports.” Two noes in one sentence say the opposite of what you mean, and mark you at once.

So choose one or the other. Say I never do sports or say I don't do sports. Both are correct, and only one of them says how often.

Watch out

The word goes in front of the verb, not at the end. Say “I sometimes play tennis.” Never “I play tennis sometimes.” as your normal sentence. Your own language ends with it. English does not.

With be it goes after, and only with be. Say “I'm always tired.” Never “I always am tired.”

One no in the sentence, never two. Say “I never do sports.” Never “I don't never do sports.”

The moves

Ask which verb it is, then place the word. The frame does not change; only the blue words do.

in front of the verb

  • I [always, usually] [work, walk] in the morning.
  • She [often, sometimes] [meets, calls] friends.
  • We [never] [watch, start] a movie on Monday.
  • They [usually] [go, arrive] early.

after the verb be

  • I'm [always, never] [tired, bored].
  • She's [usually, often] [late, early].
  • They're [sometimes] [here, busy].
  • It's [never] [open, quiet] on Monday.

Ask which verb it is. With be the word goes after; with every other verb it goes in front.

With be, and with every other verb

with be, after

I'm never bored.

with any other verb, in front

I never do sports.

She usually goes running.

Why this works

How often is half of what you say about your own life, and it is the first thing anybody asks you about your week. The words themselves take ten minutes. The position takes longer, because your own language will keep pulling the word to the end of the sentence. Put it in front of the verb until it stops feeling wrong.
