PROFESSIONAL ENGLISH PRACTICE  ·  A2  ·  WHAT WAS HAPPENING

Past continuous

What was already happening when something else arrived.

ACCESS

Goal. A call that nobody answered, and four reasons why. Listen and read at the same time, and find the one sentence that has two different past tenses in it.

ANALYSIS

Goal. A form you can already build, the one reason to use it, and the word that joins it to the other past tense.

ONE QUESTION RUNS THIS WHOLE UNIT

Was it already running?

If it was, use this tense. If it arrived and was over, use unit 21's. I was watching a movie was already running; you texted arrived. One sentence can hold both, and usually does.

THE FORM

I, he, she, it →was + verb-ingI was cooking dinner.
you, we, they →were + verb-ingThe kids were doing their homework.
to ask →was / were in front of the personWhat were you making?
to say no →wasn't / weren't + verb-ingI wasn't watching a movie.
the long thing →this tenseI was watching a movie
the short thing →unit 21's tense, after whenwhen you texted back

Two words, and you own both of them

There is nothing new in the form. Take was or were from unit 20 and put unit 13's -ing after it.

Mia says “I was cooking dinner” and “the kids were doing their homework.” Same two words, two persons.

Choose the first word by the person, exactly as in unit 20. The -ing never changes at all.

So build it in that order: the person, then was or were, then the verb with the ending.

Use it for what was already running

This is the only reason the tense exists, and it is worth more than the form.

Something was going on, and it had already started before the moment you are talking about. Mia was in the middle of cooking when the phone rang.

Compare the two: I cooked dinner says the whole thing, from start to end. I was cooking dinner says you walked in on it.

So ask whether it was already running at that moment. If it was, use this. If it started and finished there, use unit 21.

When joins the long thing to the short one

The two past tenses meet in one sentence more often than either appears alone, and when is what joins them.

Sam says “I was watching a movie when you texted back.” The long one in this tense, the short one in unit 21's.

When goes in front of the short thing. You can also start with it: When you texted back, I was watching a movie.

So put when next to the verb that arrived. Never “I was watching a movie when you were texting” about one text.

To ask and to say no, unit 20 again

The first of the two words does all the work, and it is the verb you met in unit 1 and again in unit 20.

To ask, put it in front of the person: Sam asks “What were you making?”

To say no, put not after it: I wasn't watching a movie. They weren't working.

So use no helper anywhere. Never “Did you were cooking?”

Watch out

Two words, and the first one carries the person. Say “I was cooking.” Never “I cooking.” and never “I were cooking.”

The short thing takes the other tense. Say “when you texted.” Never “when you were texting” about one text that arrived and was over.

No helper, in the question or in the no. Say “Were you cooking?” Never “Did you were cooking?”

The moves

Ask whether it was already running, then say it. The frame does not change; only the blue words do.

what was already running

  • I [was] [cooking, working] at eight.
  • The kids [were] [doing, watching] [their homework, a movie].
  • She [was] [waiting, reading] outside.
  • We [weren't] [listening, working].

the short thing that arrived

  • I was cooking when the phone [rang, stopped].
  • She was reading when somebody [knocked, called].
  • They were working when the [noise, problem] started.
  • [What, Who] [were] you [making, doing]?

Why this works

Every story you tell about yesterday has a background and an event, and this tense is the background. Without it you can only list things. With it you can say what you were in the middle of when the phone rang, which is how people actually explain themselves.
