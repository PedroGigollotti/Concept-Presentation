ANALYSIS
------------------------------------------------------------------------
Goal. Learn the six words for how often. Then learn where to put them.

THE STRUCTURE
These six words say how often. They go in one place. It is not the end of the sentence. Put them BEFORE the main verb: “She usually works here”. With “am”, “is” and “are”, put them AFTER: “She is usually here”. That is the whole rule.
   | before the main verb  ·  Form: person + adverb + verb  ·  Meaning: How often he does it.  ·  Use it to: say how often somebody does something  ·  example: She usually works here.
   | after am, is, are  ·  Form: person + is + adverb  ·  Meaning: How often it is true.  ·  Use it to: say how often something is so  ·  example: She is usually here.
   | always to never  ·  Form: always, usually, often, sometimes, rarely, never  ·  Meaning: From every time to no time.  ·  Use it to: say how much of the time  ·  example: He never works on Friday.

WATCH OUT

   [ Not at the end. Say “She usually works on Monday.” Never say “× She works usually on Monday.” This unit exists for that mistake. Never is already negative. Say “He never works on Friday.” Never say “× He doesn't never work.” One negative is enough. You will hear hardly ever. “Hardly ever” and “rarely” sit in the same place on the scale. People say “hardly ever” more often. “He rarely calls” is “He hardly ever calls”. ]

   [ The other way to say it. “Every day”, “twice a week” and “on Monday” say how often too. Those go at the END: “She works here every day”. The six words above go before the verb. Both are correct and they sit in different places. ]

THE MOVES
Ready-made lines for how often. Change only the words in blue.
   - She [usually] works on Monday.
   - He is [always] late.
   - I [never] work on Friday.
   - How often [is the meeting]?
   - We [sometimes] meet in the small room.

FROM EVERY TIME TO NO TIME
The six words sit in an order. The order is how much of the time.
   (figure) Six words, one order. Nothing between them is a different kind of word.

WHY THIS WORKS
Your language puts these words almost anywhere and everybody still understands. English keeps one place for them. Put it at the end and nobody corrects you. Miss the “never” and you come in on Friday. The meeting is not there.

A. How often?
   Listen. Is it every time, some of the time, or no time? Write every, some or none.
   1. (heard, not printed) “She always works on Monday.”
      -> every
   2. (heard, not printed) “He never works on Friday.”
      -> none
   3. (heard, not printed) “She sometimes works late.”
      -> some
   4. (heard, not printed) “He is always here at nine.”
      -> every
   5. (heard, not printed) “She rarely uses the phone.”
      -> some
   6. (heard, not printed) “He never brings the coffee.”
      -> none

B. Spot and fix the error
   Each sentence has one mistake. Write it correctly.
   1. × She usually is late.
      -> She is usually late.
   2. × He doesn't never work here.
      -> He never works here.
   3. × Always she works on Monday.
      -> She always works on Monday.
   4. × We meet sometimes.
      -> We sometimes meet.
   5. × He is often not here.
      -> He often isn't here.

C. Put the word in its place
   Write the sentence again with the word in brackets.
   1. She works here. (usually)
      -> She usually works here.
   2. He is late. (always)
      -> He is always late.
   3. I work late. (never)
      -> I never work late.
   4. They bring coffee. (sometimes)
      -> They sometimes bring coffee.
   5. She is in the office. (rarely)
      -> She is rarely in the office.
   6. We meet in the small room. (often)
      -> We often meet in the small room.

D. Before or after?
   Look at the verb. Choose the English.
   1. ( She usually works / She works usually ) on Monday.
      -> She usually works
   2. ( He is always / He always is ) late.
      -> He is always
   3. ( They never meet / They meet never ) on Friday.
      -> They never meet
   4. ( She is often / She often is ) in the small room.
      -> She is often
   5. ( I sometimes bring / I bring sometimes ) the coffee.
      -> I sometimes bring
   6. ( He is rarely / He rarely is ) here at nine.
      -> He is rarely

E. How much of the time?
   Read the word. Choose what it means.
   1. always
      -> b. every time, with nothing left out
   2. usually
      -> d. almost every time
   3. often
      -> c. a lot of the time, but not all
   4. sometimes
      -> f. only now and then
   5. rarely
      -> e. a little of the time
   6. never
      -> a. no time at all
   the endings as the student sees them:
      a. no time at all
      b. every time, with nothing left out
      c. a lot of the time, but not all
      d. almost every time
      e. a little of the time
      f. only now and then
