ANALYSIS

Goal. Learn to ask questions with to be. Learn to say no. Then ask, answer, and correct.

The structure

Questions and negatives are easy. You already know am, is, and are. For a negative, add not after the verb. For a question, put the verb before the subject. The words stay the same. Only the order changes.

Negatives

Form
subject + am / is / are + not
Meaning
Add not to say what is not true.
Use it to
correct something, or say no
She isn't here.

Yes / no questions

Form
am / is / are + subject?
Meaning
Put the verb first to ask a yes or no question.
Use it to
check something
Are you Ana?

where, who, what

Form
where / who / what + is / are + subject?
Meaning
The answer tells you the word: Where is the meeting? → In room 4. Who is she? → She is my manager. What is it? → It is a problem.
Use it to
ask for a place, a person, or a thing
Where is the meeting?

Watch out

In a question, the verb comes first. Ask “Is she ready?” The word order makes it a question, not just your voice.

Short answers keep the verb. Say “Yes, I am,” not just “Yes.” Say “No, she isn't,” not just “No.”

Never end on the short form. Say “Yes, I am,” never “× Yes, I'm.” The short form cannot end a sentence.

Short forms. A negative has two short forms. She isn't and she's not are both correct. I'm not has only one. There is no amn't in English.

The moves

Ready-made lines for checking something. Change only the words in blue.

  • Are you [the new designer]?
  • Is the meeting [in room 4]?
  • Where is [the office]?
  • I'm not [late].
  • They aren't [ready].

What moves, and what does not

One sentence, three shapes. Only the order of the first two words changes. Not comes after the verb.

Why this works

English does not ask a question with your voice alone. It asks with word order. You hear Is she ready? The verb is first. So the question is clear at once. One mistake is very common. People keep the statement order and lift the voice: She is ready? It sounds unfinished. Move the verb to the front. Then it sounds right.
