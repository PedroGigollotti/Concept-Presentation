ANALYSIS
------------------------------------------------------------------------
Goal. Learn to say the hour in English. Then say which half of the day you mean.

THE STRUCTURE
The English clock has twelve hours, never twenty-four. Say the hour, then the minutes: “nine thirty”. Nine thirty comes twice a day. So English adds “a.m.” for the first half. It adds “p.m.” for the second.
   | the hour, then the minutes  ·  Form: hour + minutes  ·  Meaning: The time, on a clock of twelve.  ·  Use it to: say any time  ·  example: It is nine thirty.
   | a.m. and p.m.  ·  Form: time + a.m. or p.m.  ·  Meaning: “A.m.” is the first half of the day. “P.m.” is the second half.  ·  Use it to: say which nine thirty you mean  ·  example: It is nine thirty p.m.
   | past and to  ·  Form: half past / a quarter past / a quarter to + hour  ·  Meaning: The same time, in the way people say it.  ·  Use it to: sound ordinary  ·  example: It is half past nine.

WATCH OUT

   [ Twelve, not twenty-four. Say “nine thirty p.m.” Never say “twenty-one thirty.” In English the second half of the clock is a letter. It is not a number. Noon and midnight are not twelve a.m. and twelve p.m. Say “twelve noon” and “twelve midnight.” Never say “× twelve a.m.” or “× twelve p.m.” ]

   [ Watch these. “A quarter past nine” is nine fifteen. “A quarter to ten” is nine forty-five. “To” names the NEXT hour. It catches people every day. ]

THE MOVES
Ready-made lines for saying the time. Change only the words in blue.
   - It is [nine thirty].
   - The meeting is at [nine thirty a.m.]
   - It is half past [nine].
   - Is it [a.m.] or [p.m.]?
   - It is [twelve noon].

TWO NINE THIRTIES
   (figure) The number is the same in both halves. Only the letters are different.

WHY THIS WORKS
Your clock has twenty-four hours. The English one has twelve. It runs twice. Every hour after noon has a number from your own clock. It also has a letter. Say twenty-one thirty. Nobody is rude about it. They are just not there at nine thirty at night.
