ANALYSIS
------------------------------------------------------------------------
Goal. Learn the four pointing words. Then say which thing you mean, and whose it is.

THE STRUCTURE
A pointing word answers two questions at once. Near you or far from you. One thing or more than one. Near and one is “this”. Near and more is “these”. Far and one is “that”. Far and more is “those”. Then add the owner with “'s”: “Lena's keys”.
   | this and these (near you)  ·  Form: this + one thing / these + more than one  ·  Meaning: Near you.  ·  Use it to: point at something near you  ·  example: This book is Ana's.
   | that and those (far from you)  ·  Form: that + one thing / those + more than one  ·  Meaning: Far from you.  ·  Use it to: point across the table  ·  example: Those socks are Bruno's.
   | the owner with's  ·  Form: a person +'s + the thing  ·  Meaning: Whose it is.  ·  Use it to: give a thing back  ·  example: That is Lena's bag.

WATCH OUT

   [ Match the word to the number. Say “this pen” for one and “these pens” for more. Never say “× this pens.” This unit exists for that mistake. Near or far, not big or small. “These” and “those” are about distance from you. They say nothing about size. ]

   [ Watch this one. The thing does not need a second mention. “This one is mine.” “Those are Lena's.” The pointing word carries the noun with it. ]

NEAR OR FAR, ONE OR MORE
   (figure) Answer the two questions and the word is already chosen. Then the owner takes an “'s”.

WHY THIS WORKS
A pointing word does two jobs in one short word. “Those” already says not near me, and more than one. On a table of lost property it is the whole message. Get the number wrong. Then the other person looks at the wrong thing.
