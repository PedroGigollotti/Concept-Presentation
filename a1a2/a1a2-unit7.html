ANALYSIS

Goal. Learn the four pointing words. Then say which thing you mean, and whose it is.

The structure

A pointing word answers two questions at once. Near you or far from you. One thing or more than one. Near and one is this. Near and more is these. Far and one is that. Far and more is those. Then add the owner with 's: Lena's keys.

this and these (near you)

Form
this + one thing / these + more than one
Meaning
Near you.
Use it to
point at something near you
This book is Ana's.

that and those (far from you)

Form
that + one thing / those + more than one
Meaning
Far from you.
Use it to
point across the table
Those socks are Bruno's.

the owner with 's

Form
a person + 's + the thing
Meaning
Whose it is.
Use it to
give a thing back
That is Lena's bag.

Watch out

Match the word to the number. Say “this pen” for one and “these pens” for more. Never say “× this pens.” This unit exists for that mistake.

Near or far, not big or small. These and those are about distance from you. They say nothing about size.

Watch this one. The thing does not need a second mention. This one is mine. Those are Lena's. The pointing word carries the noun with it.

The moves

Ready-made lines for pointing. Change only the words in blue.

  • [This] is Lena's bag.
  • [Those] keys are Bruno's.
  • Is [this] your scarf?
  • [These] gloves are mine.
  • That is [Ana]'s book.

Near or far, one or more

Answer the two questions and the word is already chosen. Then the owner takes an 's.

Why this works

A pointing word does two jobs in one short word. Those already says not near me, and more than one. On a table of lost property it is the whole message. Get the number wrong. Then the other person looks at the wrong thing.
