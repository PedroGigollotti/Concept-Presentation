ANALYSIS
------------------------------------------------------------------------
Goal. Learn the tense for what is happening now. Then keep it apart from what happens every week.

THE STRUCTURE
This tense takes two words, not one. First “am”, “is” or “are”. Then the verb with “-ing”: “She is writing”. Unit 10 has the tense for every week. This is the tense for this minute. The two are not the same sentence.
   | am, is, are + -ing  ·  Form: person + am / is / are + verb-ing  ·  Meaning: It is happening now.  ·  Use it to: say what somebody is doing at this moment  ·  example: She is writing the report.
   | the negative  ·  Form: person + am / is / are + not + verb-ing  ·  Meaning: It is not happening now.  ·  Use it to: say what is not happening  ·  example: He isn't typing.
   | the question  ·  Form: Am / Is / Are + person + verb-ing?  ·  Meaning: You are asking about now.  ·  Use it to: ask what somebody is doing  ·  example: What are you doing?

WATCH OUT

   [ Do not drop am, is or are. Say “She is writing.” Never say “× She writing.” The “-ing” word never stands on its own. This unit exists for that mistake. Now, not every week. Say “She is writing it now.” For every week, use unit 10: “She writes the reports on Thursday.” The two say different things about the same person. Watch the spelling. Most verbs just add “-ing”: “work”, “working”. A verb ending in “e” drops it: “make”, “making”; “write”, “writing”. A short verb doubles the last letter: “run”, “running”; “sit”, “sitting”. ]

   [ Two ways to say it. “She is writing” and “She's writing” are both correct. The short one is what people say. The same goes for “is not” and “isn't”. ]

EVERY WEEK, OR RIGHT NOW
One is the whole week. One is this minute.
   (figure) She writes is every week. She is writing is this minute, and it stops.

WHY THIS WORKS
Every day somebody asks where a thing is. The answer is a habit or a minute. Say it wrong and nobody stops you. Hear it wrong and you come back on Thursday. The report is ready in ten minutes.
