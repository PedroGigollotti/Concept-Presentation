ANALYSIS
------------------------------------------------------------------------
Goal. Learn to ask questions with “to be”. Learn to say no. Then ask, answer, and correct.

THE STRUCTURE
Questions and negatives are easy. You already know “am”, “is”, and “are”. For a negative, add “not” after the verb. For a question, put the verb before the subject. The words stay the same. Only the order changes.
   | Negatives  ·  Form: subject + am / is / are + “not”  ·  Meaning: Add “not” to say what is not true.  ·  Use it to: correct something, or say no  ·  example: She isn't here.
   | Yes / no questions  ·  Form: am / is / are + subject?  ·  Meaning: Put the verb first to ask a yes or no question.  ·  Use it to: check something  ·  example: Are you Ana?
   | where, who, what  ·  Form: where / who / what + is / are + subject?  ·  Meaning: The answer tells you the word: “Where is the meeting?” → In room 4. “Who is she?” → She is my manager. “What is it?” → It is a problem.  ·  Use it to: ask for a place, a person, or a thing  ·  example: Where is the meeting?

WATCH OUT

   [ In a question, the verb comes first. Ask “Is she ready?” The word order makes it a question, not just your voice. Short answers keep the verb. Say “Yes, I am,” not just “Yes.” Say “No, she isn't,” not just “No.” Never end on the short form. Say “Yes, I am,” never “× Yes, I'm.” The short form cannot end a sentence. ]

   [ Short forms. A negative has two short forms. “She isn't” and “she's not” are both correct. “I'm not” has only one. There is no “amn't” in English. ]

THE MOVES
Ready-made lines for checking something. Change only the words in blue.
   - Are you [the new designer]?
   - Is the meeting [in room 4]?
   - Where is [the office]?
   - I'm not [late].
   - They aren't [ready].

WHAT MOVES, AND WHAT DOES NOT
   (figure) One sentence, three shapes. Only the order of the first two words changes. “Not” comes after the verb.

WHY THIS WORKS
English does not ask a question with your voice alone. It asks with word order. You hear “Is she ready?” The verb is first. So the question is clear at once. One mistake is very common. People keep the statement order and lift the voice: “She is ready?” It sounds unfinished. Move the verb to the front. Then it sounds right.

ACTIVATION
------------------------------------------------------------------------
Goal. Now practice. Build questions. Make negatives. Give short answers. Then ask a real person.
   In pairs. Ask and answer five yes or no questions with “to be”. Ask about names, jobs, and where things are. Give short answers: “Yes, I am.” “No, it isn't.” Then, on your own. Write two questions for a new colleague. Write one true negative sentence about yourself: “I'm not from here.” Read them out loud.

   1. × I amn't late.
      -> I'm not late.
   2. × Where the meeting is?
      -> Where is the meeting?
   3. × They isn't here.
      -> They aren't here.
   4. × Is you the new teacher?
      -> Are you the new teacher?
   5. × She not is ready.
      -> She isn't ready.

C. Ask the question
   Ana is the nurse. Marco is the designer. Change each sentence into a yes or no question.
   1. The nurse is from Canada.
      -> Is she from Canada?
   2. The designer is ready.
      -> Is he ready?
   3. The nurse and the designer are early.
      -> Are they early?
   4. I am late.
      -> Am I late?
   5. You are the new teacher.
      -> Are you the new teacher?

D. Choose the English question
   Look at the first two words. Choose the English question.

E. What do you say?
   Read what you hear. Choose what you say.
   1. ( She is ready? / Is she ready? )
      -> Is she ready?
   2. ( Are they early? / They are early? )
      -> Are they early?
   3. ( Is it ready? / It is ready? )
      -> Is it ready?
   4. ( You are the new teacher? / Are you the new teacher? )
      -> Are you the new teacher?
   5. ( Am I late? / I am late? )
      -> Am I late?
   6. ( He is sure? / Is he sure? )
      -> Is he sure?

F. Over to you
   Not graded. Here the grammar stops being a rule. It becomes a question you ask.
