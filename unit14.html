14 · IN: submission, arrival, and collapse

Handing things over, arriving on time, and refusing to fold.

ACCESS

Read the scene and notice the moves in bold. One of them, pour in, is never taught in this book. Before you read on, let the particle show you what it means.

A newsroom on deadline, the last hour before the pages lock.

Nadia Copy deadline is six. Who still hasn't turned in the council story?

Cole Me. I'll hand it in by five thirty. I'm confirming one quote.

Nadia Good. Did the photos come in from the stringer yet?

Cole Not yet. But reader emails poured in after the morning story.

Nadia Chase those tomorrow. Tonight the publisher wants a softer headline, and we don't cave in on the facts.

Cole Agreed. I'll put in a records request before I leave, so it's logged tonight.

ANALYSIS

In is the same hub you met in Unit 13: inside a boundary. This one takes the senses where crossing the line means handing something over to the people inside, arriving there yourself, or collapsing inward under load.

In the submission sense, you deliver something across to whoever is inside in charge: hand in a draft, turn in a report. In the arrival sense, someone or something crosses the threshold and is here: check in, come in, drop in. In the collapse sense, a structure or a will gives way and folds inward: cave in, give in, fall in.

The same wheel as Unit 13, turned. This unit takes the three solid spokes; the dotted ones were Unit 13.

Watch out. Turn in has two senses: submit a report, or go to bed. And give in is not give up: give in means yield to pressure; give up means quit or stop trying.
American / British English. American English turns in an assignment (submits it) and turns in at night (goes to bed). British English uses hand in for the assignment. This book uses turn in for submitting.

Hold in means across the line to the inside, and the only question is what crosses. A draft is handed across to the desk, a person or a photo arrives, or a wall folds inward under load. This is why hand in feels like relief and cave in feels like defeat, though they share a particle.

send in: to submit something to the people in charge. Submission spoke.

roll in: to arrive, often late or in a relaxed way. Arrival spoke.

close in: to press inward and tighten, the way walls or a deadline close in. Collapse spoke.
