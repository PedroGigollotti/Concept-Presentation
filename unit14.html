ANALYSIS

ONE QUESTION RUNS THIS WHOLE UNIT

Who is inside, and what reaches them?

Unit 13 crossed the boundary to add or absorb. This unit crosses it toward the people already inside: something is handed over to them, somebody arrives among them, or a structure folds inward under load.

The structure

In is the same hub you met in Unit 13: inside a boundary. This one takes the senses where crossing the line means handing something over to the people inside, arriving there yourself, or collapsing inward under load.

In the submission sense, you deliver something across to whoever is inside in charge: hand in a draft, turn in a report. In the arrival sense, someone or something crosses the threshold and is here: check in, come in, drop in. In the collapse sense, a structure or a will gives way and folds inward: cave in, give in, fall in.

Handing in, and giving in

Two senses in this unit involve giving something up, and they are not the same act at all.

Hand in a report and you are doing what was asked: the boundary is an office and you are complying with a process. Give in and you are doing what you resisted: the boundary is your own position and it has just collapsed.

The picture is identical — something crosses inward. What differs is whether there was resistance before it crossed, and that is what makes collapse the only one of the three that carries a judgment.

The test you can run with no book

Ask: was there resistance before this?

If there was, and it stopped, the sense is collapse. If there was not, and something was delivered to an authority, it is submission. If nothing was delivered and somebody simply got here, it is arrival.

Arrival is the one that gives itself away in the grammar: it takes no object. They came in, she checked in, the emails poured in.

The mechanical half: collapse needs something that could have held out. A price does not cave in; a person or an organization defending one does.

Watch out. Turn in has two senses: submit a report, or go to bed. And give in is not give up: give in means yield to pressure; give up means quit or stop trying.
American / British English. American English turns in an assignment (submits it) and turns in at night (goes to bed). British English uses hand in for the assignment. This book uses turn in for submitting.

Hold in means across the line to the inside, and the only question is what crosses. A draft is handed across to the desk, a person or a photo arrives, or a wall folds inward under load. This is why hand in feels like relief and cave in feels like defeat, though they share a particle.

Your prediction: pour in. You predicted this one from in before you read any of the unit: they arrive from outside, in quantity and without being sent for. That is the arrival sense, and the particle is what told you.
