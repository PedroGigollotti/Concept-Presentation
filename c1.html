Professional English Practice: C1

Thirty-six units. Study the Access and Analysis sections, then work through the interactive exercises. Search for the grammar you need, or work straight through.

This is Book 3 of Professional English Practice, calibrated to C1: accurate, fluent, workplace-ready English for the whole professional world, not only executives. Every unit is built for a junior developer and a senior lead alike, and every example is drawn from the situations that actually fill a working week: standups, retros, client calls, negotiations, feedback, and the messages that get typed under real time pressure.

Thirty-six units carry you from the verb system through modality, emphasis, cohesion, reporting, and register, ending with the reformulation and lexical control that mark real fluency. Each one stands on its own, so you can work the book start to finish or open directly to the topic in front of you this week.

How each unit runs. Every unit follows the same four-part cycle, so the shape of the book never surprises you once you know it. Access: read a short, authentic workplace scene, with the target grammar already there in bold. You are noticing the pattern, not producing it yet. Analysis: learn exactly how the structure is built, what it means, and how shifting it changes your tone, ending with a visual scale or timeline so the idea has a shape, not just a rule. Activation: a full bank of exercises moving from mechanical accuracy to real, personal production, ending with a paired speaking task and a written task of your own. Answers: in this interactive edition, the key is built into each exercise. Press Check my answers and the correct form appears beside anything you missed.

Two ways to work through it. Start to finish, for a full learning path through the grammar of professional English. Or unit by unit, jumping straight to whatever you need this week using the search field below.

Do not skip the final exercise. Do the pair work and the personal writing task in every unit. That is where the grammar stops being a rule you know and becomes a sentence you actually say.

I started The Concept Method because I kept meeting brilliant professionals who could read a technical spec in English without blinking, and then freeze in a retro when they needed to disagree with their manager gently, or soften a piece of bad news, or make a joke land in a language that was not the one they grew up thinking in.

That gap is not a knowledge problem. It is a grammar problem hiding as a confidence problem. The structures in this book, the mixed conditional you use to assign blame kindly in a postmortem, the hedge that lets you say something true without overpromising, the cleft sentence that puts the one fact that matters at the front of the room, are not academic curiosities. They are the actual tools fluent speakers reach for without noticing, every single day, under real pressure.

So that is what this book teaches: not English for its own sake, but English for the Tuesday afternoon meeting where it actually counts. Every scene in this book is something that could happen at your job this week, because it is exactly the kind of moment this method was built for.

Mind your future. It is closer than you think, and it is going to need you to say the right thing at the right time.

Pedro Gigollotti NettoFounder, The Concept Method
