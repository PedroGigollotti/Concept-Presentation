ANALYSIS

Goal. Learn the verbs where the form after them changes the fact reported, so a status answer says what actually happened.

ONE QUESTION RUNS THIS WHOLE UNIT

Which fact am I reporting?

With these verbs the choice is not style. To plus a verb and the -ing form report different events, and the listener acts on whichever you said.

THE FORM

Remember / forgetremember/forget + verb-ing (past memory); remember/forget + to + verb (a duty)
Stop / trystop + verb-ing (end an activity); stop + to + verb (pause to start another); try + verb-ing (experiment); try + to + verb (attempt)I stopped calling after the second attempt. I stopped to check the total before I sent it. Watch out Don't swap stop doing and stop to do. “I stopped to smoke” means you paused another activity in order to smoke. “I stopped smoking” means you quit smoking entirely. These are opposite claims.

Four verbs where the form carries the fact

Most verbs take one shape or the other and nothing depends on it. These four are different, and they turn up in status conversations constantly.

Remember to do is the task; remember doing is the memory. Stop to do is interrupting for a purpose; stop doing is ending the activity.

Try to do is attempting something hard; try doing is testing a method. Regret to say is announcing bad news; regret saying is wishing you had not.

The test you can run with no book

Ask: which fact am I reporting? Then say it the long way and see which one is true.

Did the sending happen? - that is remember to send. Do I have a memory of sending? - that is remember sending. Priya's colleague asked the first and got the second, which is why she adds actually, last Tuesday to close the gap.

The mechanical half: the -ing form always points backwards, the to form always points forwards. That holds for all four pairs and is the single thing worth memorizing.

The pairs in a working conversation

I stopped to check the total before I sent it reports care taken. I stopped calling after the second attempt reports a decision to give up on a channel.

Both appear in Priya's four sentences, and swapping either one would report something she did not do.

This is why the pair matters more than most grammar points at C1: the error is invisible - both versions are perfectly formed - and the fact reported is wrong.

The verbs that do not care

Begin, start, continue, like, hate: with these, both forms mean the same and the choice is rhythm.

It started to rain and it started raining report the same event.

Knowing which list a verb is on is the actual work. The four in this unit are the ones that cost you accuracy; the rest cost you nothing.

Watch out

Answer the question that was asked. Did you remember to send it? asks whether it went. I remember sending it answers a different question, so add the detail that closes the gap: I remember sending it, last Tuesday.

Don't apply these pairs to verbs that don't take them. Begin, start and continue mean the same in both forms. The meaning change belongs to remember, stop, try, regret and a short list beside them.

The moves

Same verb, different meaning, in every line. The highlighted slot changes; the complement always decides which meaning applies.

Reporting the task

  • Did you remember to [send the invoice / copy legal]?
  • I stopped to [check the total / read it once more] before I sent it.
  • I tried to [reach the client / get an answer] all afternoon.

Reporting what you did

  • I remember [sending it / attaching it], last Tuesday.
  • I stopped [calling / chasing it] after the second attempt.
  • I tried [calling twice / emailing instead], no luck.

Same verb, opposite direction in time, depending on what follows it.

Why this works

These pairs are dangerous precisely because both versions are correct English: nobody corrects you, and the fact you reported is wrong. In a status conversation that is expensive - a colleague hears that you stopped checking when you stopped to check, or that you have a memory of sending when they asked whether it went out. The forms are easy; the discipline is asking which fact you are reporting before you choose, and these four verbs come up often enough that it has to become automatic.
