17 · Verb patterns: -ing and infinitive

The exact verb for the exact shade.

ACCESS

Jiro (team lead) Rea, walk Toby through the handover.

Rea And I remember sending the Q2 archive to legal, so that's done.

Toby (incoming analyst) And if the sync fails?

ANALYSIS

Goal. Learn the meaning-change verbs and the fixed complementation patterns, and how the complement carries the meaning. By the end you should be able to pick the exact form for the exact shade.

ONE QUESTION RUNS THIS WHOLE UNIT

Am I pointing forward at a task, or back at a fact?

The to form points forward. The -ing form points back. That holds for all of these verbs and is the only thing worth memorising.

The structure

Many English verbs can be followed by either a to-infinitive or an -ing form, and for a whole set of them the choice changes the meaning, sometimes completely. “I stopped to check the logs” means you paused in order to check; “I stopped checking the logs” means you gave up checking. Remember to send it is a task for the future; remember sending it is a memory of the past. Beyond these meaning-change verbs, there is a wider skill: knowing the fixed pattern each verb takes. Suggest and recommend take -ing or a that clause but never a plain to (suggest meeting, not suggest to meet). These patterns are not logical; you learn them, and getting one slightly wrong is one of the clearest signs of a non-native speaker.

Meaning-change verbs

Form
remember / forget / stop / regret + to-infinitive vs + -ing, with different meanings
Meaning
the two forms mean genuinely different things
Use it to
say precisely which meaning you intend
stopped to check (paused in order to) vs stopped checking (quit); remember to send (future task) vs remember sending (past memory)

try / mean / go on

Form
try to (attempt) vs try -ing (experiment); mean to (intend) vs mean -ing (involve); go on to (next thing) vs go on -ing (continue)
Meaning
each pairing splits into two distinct senses
Use it to
control the exact relationship between the two actions
Try to restart it (attempt) vs Try restarting it (experiment); This will mean working weekends (involve) vs I meant to call (intended)

Fixed complementation patterns

Form
suggest / recommend + -ing or that; advise + object + to; make / let + object + base; help + (to)
Meaning
the pattern is fixed by the verb, and errors sound non-native
Use it to
complement each verb the way it demands
suggest revising (never suggest to revise); advised us to wait; made us wait; helped (to) fix it

Four verbs where the form carries the fact

Most verbs take one shape and nothing depends on it. These four are different, and a handover is exactly where they turn up.

Remember to do is the task; remember doing is the memory. Stop to do is interrupting for a purpose; stop doing is ending the activity.

Try to do is attempting something hard; try doing is testing a method. Regret to say is delivering bad news now; regret saying is wishing you had not.

The test you can run with no book

Ask: am I pointing forward or back? The to form goes forward, the -ing form goes back. That single direction settles all four pairs.

Rea uses both in consecutive lines and the direction is what separates them: the Friday report is ahead of Toby, the Q2 archive is behind her.

The mechanical half is that these are correct English either way, so nothing will flag the wrong one. A spellchecker, a grammar checker and a colleague reading quickly will all pass a sentence that reports the opposite of what you did.

Why a handover is the worst place to get it wrong

A handover is a list of things that are done and things that are not, and these verbs are how the list is written.

I remember sending the archive closes an item. Remember to send the archive opens one. Swap them and the incoming analyst either re-sends something legal already has or never sends something they are waiting for.

Neither error surfaces until weeks later, which is why this is worth drilling past the point where you have to think about it.

The verbs that do not care

Begin, start, continue, like, prefer, hate: with these both forms mean the same and the choice is rhythm.

It started to rain and it started raining report one event. Nothing is at stake.

So the work is not learning a rule about -ing and to. It is knowing which short list of verbs makes the distinction, and those are the four above plus a handful beside them.

Watch out

Meaning-change verbs are not interchangeable. Remember to lock up and remember locking up are different claims; picking the wrong one misinforms. Choose by meaning, not by ear.

Suggest and recommend don't take a bare to + infinitive. They take -ing, a that-clause, or a noun: suggest meeting, suggest that we meet, not suggest to meet. With recommend, British English also allows an object plus an infinitive, recommend you to see someone; American English uses the that-clause.

American / British English.

Help + verb. Both varieties allow help (to) do; American English more often drops the to (helped fix it). This book uses the bare form (help fix).

Go + verb. American English says go see, come sit (bare); British English uses go and see. This book uses go and see in formal writing.

The moves

Same verb, exact shade. Keep the frame; change only the slot and the situation.

  • Remember to [send / cc] the client.
  • Try [restarting / clearing] the cache.
  • I regret to [inform / tell] you.
  • This will mean [working / traveling] weekends.
  • We suggest [revising / delaying] the plan.

Where each move sits

Same verb, opposite meanings. The complement, to or -ing, decides which you mean.

One more -ing looks like the same rule and is not. In look forward to and be used to, the to is a preposition, not part of an infinitive, and a preposition takes -ing: look forward to hearing from you, be used to working late. Nothing about infinitives is involved, which is why the two groups behave alike for different reasons.

Why this works

Here is why this small corner of grammar deserves real attention. Most of the time, the wrong structure just sounds a little off. With the meaning-change verbs, it actually says something false. If you tell a colleague remember locking the server room when you meant remember to lock it, you have turned an instruction into a claim about the past, and they may leave it unlocked. So this is about being understood, not about style. The wider patterns work differently: they rarely change the meaning, but they separate fluent from almost-fluent. I suggest to reconvene is perfectly clear, and clearly non-native, because a native ear expects I suggest reconvening. There is no rule to work it out from; the verb simply carries its pattern, so you store the pattern with the verb.

1 · Under pressure

Verb patterns are the fastest place in English to say something you did not mean. Forty seconds each, and the meaning in brackets is the whole task.

2 · Say it

These two forms are almost identical in sound and opposite in meaning, which is precisely why they need saying, not reading.

Record situations 1 and 2 one after the other. Play them back and listen for one thing: are they clearly different? Remember to lock and remember locking differ by one syllable and by an entire meaning. If a listener could mishear one for the other, put more weight on to.
