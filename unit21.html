21 · AROUND: aimlessness, circulation, avoidance, and reorientation

Kicking ideas around, spreading the word, going around blockers, and turning things around.

ACCESS

A product team at a startup, fixing an onboarding flow that keeps losing users.

Zoe The onboarding flow's a mess. Let's kick around some fixes before we commit.

Amir I poked around the analytics. Most users drop off at step three.

Zoe Good catch. Ask around the support team. They hear the complaints firsthand.

Amir There's a rendering bug at step three. I can work around it for now while we fix it properly.

Zoe Do it. If we turn this flow around, retention follows.

Amir Agreed. Let's pass the new mockups around and see if design comes around to the simpler version.

ANALYSIS

Around's hub is physical: in a circle, with no fixed target. Walk around, look around, turn around. Every abstract sense in this unit is that circling applied to loose activity, circulation among people, going around an obstacle, or turning to face a new way.

In the aimlessness sense, you do things loosely: mess around, kick around. In the circulation sense, something moves among people: pass around, ask around. In the avoidance sense, you go around an obstacle: work around, talk around. In the reorientation sense, you turn to face a new direction: turn around, come around.

The hub is the literal sense. Each spoke is a way of going in circles rather than straight. All four belong to this unit.

Watch out. Get around has three senses: move about, spread as news, or circumvent a rule. And come around is not bring around: come around means change your own mind; bring around means change someone else's mind.
American / British English. American English uses around for almost everything: turn around, ask around. British English often uses round: turn round, ask round. This book uses around throughout.

Hold “around means circling, with no straight line to one target,” and the only question is how the circling is used: tinkering without a goal, a thing moving hand to hand, going around a blockage, or turning to face a new way. This is why kick around an idea feels playful and work around a bug feels resourceful, though they share a particle.
