A, an, the, and the zero article

Three tiny words that report what your listener already knows.

ANALYSIS

Goal. Learn a, an, the and no article at all. By the end, you can pick one by asking what your listener already knows, instead of guessing.

ONE QUESTION RUNS THIS WHOLE UNIT

What does your listener already know?

Nothing yet, and it is one of several: a or an. He knows which one: the. You do not mean one thing at all: nothing.

THE FORM

new to him, one of severala / an + singular countable nounI need a laptop
he knows which onethe + any nounthe one on the desk in Room 3
not one thing, but the kindno article + plural or uncountablelaptops with bigger screens

One question, asked before every noun

Articles feel like decoration and they are not. Each one reports something about your reader.

A says: this is new to you, and it is one of several. Ivo has not chosen a laptop, so Zoe cannot picture one.

The says: you know which one, or you can work it out from where we are. Zoe points at a desk in Room 3 and Ivo follows her.

Nothing at all says: I have stopped talking about one thing. Laptops means the kind, not a machine.

The reason this is hard has nothing to do with the words. Your own language does the same job with word order and with context. Only the habit of asking is new.

New, and then known

This is the pattern that makes the two words easiest to feel, and it happens in almost every conversation.

The first time a thing appears it is new, so it takes a. I saw a cat.

The second time it is known, because you just introduced it, so it takes the. The cat was black.

Watch it happen in the scene. Ivo says a laptop, and Zoe answers with the one on the desk. By then there is a particular laptop between them.

If you learn nothing else here, learn this. Introduce with a, continue with the, and half of your articles come out right on their own.

Nothing is a choice, not an accident

The third option is to write no article at all, and it is a real decision rather than a gap.

It is available in front of a PLURAL or an UNCOUNTABLE noun, and nowhere else. Laptops are expensive. Money is the problem.

So it rests entirely on a thing you did in unit 9. If you cannot tell a noun that counts from one that does not, you cannot use this.

The meaning is always the same: the kind, in general, not a particular one of them.

And a singular countable noun can NEVER stand alone. Laptop is expensive is not a sentence, and there is no situation that rescues it.

The ones you simply learn

A small set of expressions freeze their article, and no rule predicts them.

Go to work, at home, by train, in bed take nothing. On the whole and in the end keep a the. As a rule keeps an a.

Four of the six words in your bank are exactly this. They are there so you meet them as single blocks, rather than as decisions.

One more thing that is about SOUND and not spelling. An goes before a vowel SOUND: an hour, because the h is silent. A university, because it begins with a y sound.

Say the noun out loud before you choose. Your ear is right here and the spelling is not.

Two nouns, one idea

An article sits in front of a noun. Very often it sits in front of TWO, and the first one is there to describe the second.

A sales report. The budget meeting. A customer complaint. The first noun works like an adjective, and English builds these endlessly.

One rule governs all of them. The first noun stays singular, however many it means. A contract of three years is a three-year contract. A team that handles sales is the sales team.

When two nouns will not do the job, you have two more tools. ’s goes on a person: Ana's decision, the manager's office.

Of goes on a thing: the price of the laptop, the end of the month. Reach for it second, not first.

Names, and the ones that keep an article

Names are the place where the article rule looks like it stops working. Most names take nothing at all.

France. Paris. Oxford Street. Everest. Microsoft. The name already points at one thing, so no article is needed.

But some names keep the, and they are not random. Two things put it there.

The name is PLURAL: the Netherlands, the Philippines, the Andes.

Or the name has a common noun inside it, doing the work.

The United States. The Thames. The Atlantic. The Times.

So it is the same rule after all. Where a real noun is in there, the article comes with it.

Watch out

An is about the SOUND, not the letter. Say “an hour,” never “a hour,” because the h is silent. And say “a university,” never “an university,” because it begins with a y sound.

An uncountable noun never takes a. Say “he gave me some advice,” never “he gave me a advice.” The same is true of information, news and furniture, all of which count in your own language.

The first noun stays singular. Say “a three-year contract,” never “a three-years contract.” The number is already there. The noun does not repeat it.

’s for people, of for things, and two nouns beat both. “The report of sales” breaks no rule. Nobody writes it. English reaches for the sales report first and keeps of for the cases two nouns cannot carry.

A plain country name takes nothing at all. Say “she flew to France,” never “she flew to the France.” The name is already doing the pointing.

A name built from a common noun keeps its the. Say “the United States is our biggest market,” never “United States is our biggest market.” The word states is a noun, and it wants its article.

AMERICAN AND BRITISH ENGLISH

In front of an institution, British English drops the article and American English keeps it. The rule is about the FUNCTION, not the building.

British: she is in hospital · he is at university

American: she is in the hospital · he is at the university

When you mean the building itself, both do the same thing. I parked at the hospital is normal everywhere, because you are talking about a place and not a treatment.

This book keeps the article. Recognize the other one and leave it where you found it.

The moves

The frame stays the same. You change only the blue words. The column reports what your listener is holding.

He does not know it yet

  • I need a [laptop].
  • She is an [engineer].
  • There is a [new employee] starting Monday.

He knows, or it is the kind

  • Pass me the [report].
  • The [budget] is approved.
  • [Laptops] are expensive.

The article is not about the laptop. It is about how much your listener already has in his head when the noun arrives.

Why this works

Articles are the smallest words in English and they carry a signal nothing else carries. They tell your reader whether you are introducing something or pointing at it. Get that backwards and he spends a second hunting a thing you never mentioned. Or waiting for you to explain a thing he already knows. It is a small friction and it happens in every sentence you write. Your own language does the same work with word order and with what has already been mentioned. Nothing here is a new idea. Only a new habit.

This check looks at two things only: that your expression is there, and that it looks like a sentence. IT DOES NOT LOOK AT YOUR ARTICLES AT ALL, and that is not laziness. An article is right or wrong depending on what your listener already knows, and no checker in the world knows that. This is the one exercise in the book where the model is the whole of the feedback.
