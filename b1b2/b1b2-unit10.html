PROFESSIONAL ENGLISH PRACTICE  ·  B1  ·  THE FIRST CONDITIONAL

First conditional: unless, as long as, in case

Setting the terms on something that can really happen, once or every time.

ACCESS

ANALYSIS

Goal. Learn to set a condition and mean it. By the end, you can agree to something without agreeing to everything, and choose how hard the terms sound.

ONE QUESTION RUNS THIS WHOLE UNIT

Which half is the condition?

That half takes the PRESENT, always. What the OTHER half takes depends on whether you mean one occasion or every occasion. You asked this same question in unit 8. The answer has not changed: the half with the little word takes the present.

THE FORM

the conditionif / unless / as long as + presentif you give me two people
the result, for one occasionwill or 'llI will finish by Friday
either ordercomma only when the condition comes firstIf you give me two, I'll finish.

These are things that can actually happen

Everything in this unit is about something that CAN happen. Felix may get two people. Something else may land on him. Alice may need him at the call. Nobody in that scene is imagining anything.

That is why both halves stay in ordinary tenses. The condition takes the present and the result takes will. You are talking about the real world, and the real future.

English has another kind of conditional for situations that are NOT real. It changes the tenses to say so. That one is unit 11. When you get there, the first question will be whether the situation could actually happen. That is what chooses between the two.

You have already learned the machine

Look at what Felix said. “If you give me two people, I will finish by Friday.”

One half has the present in it. The other half has will. That is exactly what unit 8 did with when and as soon as, and it is the same rule.

So the only new thing here is WHICH little word you put in front. Each one sets a different kind of term.

If is the plain one. Something may happen; here is what follows.

As long as is a condition you are holding open. Felix will finish by Friday, and the moment something else lands on him, the promise ends.

Unless reverses. I will move the call unless you need me means he moves it by default. Alice has to act to stop him, and that is a different arrangement from if.

In case is not a condition at all, and this is the one people misuse. It is a precaution. He prepares the notes whether or not she needs him, precisely because he does not know.

When both halves take the present

There is one conditional whose second half does not take will, and you will write it more than you expect.

If you heat iron, it expands. Both halves take the present. This is not about one occasion. It is about every occasion.

That is the whole difference. If you send the data today, I will finish by Friday is one Friday. If a form arrives after six, it goes in the next batch is what always happens.

Use it for rules, systems and processes. How the software behaves, what the policy does, what happens to a late form. At work you will need this one constantly.

And here when means the same as if. When you heat iron, it expands says exactly the same thing. It is the one place in English where those two words agree.

Unless is the one to slow down for

Unless means EXCEPT IF, and that is why it flips the default.

“I will call you if there is a problem” means silence is normal. You hear from him only when something goes wrong.

“I will call you unless there is a problem” means the call is normal. Silence means something went wrong.

Those two sentences arrange your week differently, and the only difference is one word.

How hard the terms sound

This is where the choice becomes yours. The four words set the same condition at four distances.

As long as is the warmest. It sounds like you want the thing to work and you are naming what would stop it.

If is neutral. It states a fact about the world.

Provided that is formal and firm. It belongs in writing, and it sounds like a term somebody will hold you to later.

Or else is a threat and almost never belongs at work. It is in your bank so you recognize it, not so you use it.

How hard you want to sound

The idea
This part is your choice. The four words hold the same condition at four distances, from an offer to a threat.
warm, and you want it to work
Friday holds as long as nothing else lands on me.
formal, and somebody will hold you to it
Provided that we have enough people, Friday holds.
Same condition. One of them is an offer and one of them is a contract.

Watch out

No will in the condition half. Say “if you give me two,” never “if you will give me two.” It is the unit 8 rule again, and it catches people again.

Unless is already negative. “Unless you need me” and “unless you don't need me” are both English, and they are opposites. Unless already carries the not. Nothing there is ungrammatical; you simply say the reverse of what you meant, and nobody will query it.

The moves

The frame stays the same. You change only the blue words. The column you pick decides whether the reader hears an offer or a term.

An offer

  • Friday holds as long as [nothing else lands].
  • I'll do it if you [give me two people].
  • I'll prepare notes in case [you need them].

A term

  • Provided that [we have enough people], Friday holds.
  • I will not start unless [the budget arrives].
  • On condition that [nothing changes], we deliver Friday.

Same two facts, one word apart. The word decides which of them is the surprise.

Why this works

Agreeing to something without conditions is how people deliver things they never promised. These four words let you say yes and stay honest at the same time. And the one you pick tells the other person how much room there is. Neither of you has to learn it the hard way.

F

Over to you

No checking here. This one is yours.

In pairs. One of you asks for something urgent. The other says yes, and sets three terms: one with as long as, one with unless, and one precaution with in case. Then swap, and this time set the same three terms with provided that, and ask your partner which version he would have signed.

On your own. Write a short reply agreeing to something you have not got time for. Set two conditions and one precaution. Read it aloud and check that you have not accidentally promised the thing outright.
