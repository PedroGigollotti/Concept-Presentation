ANALYSIS

Goal. Learn the passive at any point in time. By the end, you can report a piece of work without saying who is doing it, in whatever tense it needs.

ONE QUESTION RUNS THIS WHOLE UNIT

What tense is be in? The participle never changes.

This is not six new forms. It is one verb, be, conjugating in front of a participle you have owned since unit 16. Put be in the tense you want and stop.

THE FORM

Only be travels

Look at the four blue verbs. Have been reviewed. Is being written. Will be sent. Had been filed.

One word is the same in every one of them, and it is the last one. The participle does not move at all.

What changes is be, and it changes exactly the way it changes anywhere else in English. Is, was, has been, had been, will be, is being.

So there is nothing new to memorize here. Put be into the tense you want and put the participle after it.

That is the whole form, and it works after a modal too: must be approved, can be sent, should have been checked.

The perfects, and the future

Has been and had been do the jobs you already know them for.

“The documents have been reviewed” is a result that still counts, which is unit 2's tense wearing a passive.

“That one had already been filed before I joined” puts one past event before another, which is unit 25's.

Will be is the future one, and it is the sentence a status report ends on. “It will be sent on Friday.”

None of these is a new tense. They are the tenses you have, with the focus moved off the person doing the work.

In progress, and after a modal

Two shapes are worth a second look because they are the ones people avoid.

Is being and was being report work that is running. “The report is being written now.” It is not finished and it has not stalled.

Two forms of be land next to each other there, which is why it feels wrong at first. It is not wrong. One is the tense and one is the passive.

After a modal, be goes back to its bare form. Must be approved. Can be sent. Should be checked.

And a modal perfect takes both: should have been checked. That is unit 30's form with a passive inside it.

A status, or a claim of credit

This is where the choice becomes yours, and it is a different move from the one unit 16 taught.

Unit 16 asked whether naming the person would turn a fact into an accusation. This one asks whether naming them turns a fact into a claim.

“The documents have been reviewed” is a status. “I reviewed the documents” is a status plus a claim of credit.

In a written update the passive is usually right. The reader wants the state of the work, not a list of who did what.

In a conversation with your own manager it can read as hiding. If you did it, and the person asking needs to know that, say so.

Watch out

After a modal, be loses its tense. Say “it must be approved,” never “it must is approved” and never “it must been approved.” A modal is always followed by a bare verb.

The participle, not the past simple. Say “it has been written,” never “it has been wrote.” The same irregular list you learned for the perfects does the work here.

Being is not optional in the continuous. “The report is being written” and “the report is written” are both correct English, and they say different things. The first is work in progress. The second says it exists in written form. The two forms of be in the first one are there on purpose. Dropping being does not shorten the sentence; it changes it.

The moves

The frame stays the same. You change only the blue words. The column you pick decides where in time the work stands.

Finished, or coming

  • The documents have been [reviewed].
  • That one had already been [filed].
  • It will be [sent] on Friday.

Running, or required

  • The report is being [written] now.
  • The system was being [tested] all week.
  • It must be [approved] by legal first.

The grey chip is in the same place five times. Everything that moves is on the left.

Why this works

Most of what you write at work is a status. Somebody wants to know where a piece of work stands. Usually they do not want a list of names attached to each step. The passive is how English does that, and this unit is what lets you do it at any point in time. Finished, running, owed, required. The relief is that none of it is new. You take the tense you already wanted and put it on the word be.

F

Over to you

No checking here. This one is yours.

In pairs. Give each other a spoken status on something real: what has been done, what is being done, what will be done, what must be done first. Four sentences, four tenses, no names in any of them. Then say the same four with names and ask which version your partner would rather receive in writing.

On your own. Write a five-line status update on a piece of your own work. Use a perfect passive, a continuous passive and a modal passive, and put a date in the last line. Read it aloud and check that a stranger could tell what is still owed.
