Past simple and past continuous

Telling the story of what went wrong.

ANALYSIS

Goal. Learn to use two past tenses together. By the end, you can tell a story, and you can choose which part your listener notices.

ONE QUESTION RUNS THIS WHOLE UNIT

Which of the two things was already happening?

That one takes was or were with -ing. The other one takes the past simple. Ask this before you choose, every time.

THE FORM

I, he, she, itwas + verb-ingI was writing
you, we, theywere + verb-ingwe were running
negative and questionwasn't / were + personWere you running?

The long one and the short one

Look at what Dan said. “We were running the final test when the server crashed.”

Two things happened, and one of them happened inside the other. The test was already running. Then the crash interrupted it. The action that was already running takes was or were with -ing. The action that interrupts it takes the past simple. That is why Dan says “were running” and then “crashed.”

The action that interrupts is usually the shorter one, and that is a useful clue. It is not the rule. Look at this sentence: “While I was working in Madrid, I studied Spanish for two years.” Two years is not short. It is still the past simple, because it is the action that happens inside the other one. What decides the tense is not how long an action lasts. It is whether the action was already running, or whether it happened inside something that was.

Now try it away from the office. Your phone rings while you cook dinner. The cooking was already running. The ring interrupted it. So you say: “I was cooking dinner when my phone rang.”

When and while

Dan uses both words, and they do different jobs. He says “when the server crashed.” When points at the action that interrupts. He also says “while I was checking the cables.” While points at the action that was already running.

Here is a test you can run on any sentence, on your own. Say in the middle of in front of the action. “In the middle of checking the cables” sounds right, so that one takes while. “In the middle of the server crashing” does not sound right, so that one takes when.

When you do not need this at all

Read Dan's last line again. “Then I found it, restarted the server, and finished alone.”

Three things, one after the other. None of them happens inside another one. Nothing was already running, so there is nothing to interrupt. All three stay in the past simple, and Rita tells him to leave them there.

This matters, because the past continuous is not a better past simple. It is not the tense for long actions either. It is only for the thing that was already running. Where there is no such thing, using it is wrong.

The long one and the short one

The idea
One of the two actions was already running. The other one happens inside it and interrupts it. The one that was running takes was or were with -ing. The one that interrupts takes the past simple. It is usually shorter, and that is a clue, not the rule.
from the scene
we were running the final test · the server crashed
from your own week
I was cooking dinner · my phone rang
We were running the final test when the server crashed.

When and while

The idea
The two words do not do the same job. When points at the action that interrupts. While points at the action that was already running. Put them in the wrong place and your listener has to guess which action was there first.
the test you can run alone
Say in the middle of in front of the action. If it sounds right, the word is while.
from the scene
when the server crashed · while I was checking the cables
While I was checking the cables, the client left.

When you do not need it

The idea
Three things happen, one after the other. None of them happens inside another one. Nothing was already running, so there is nothing to interrupt, and every verb stays in the past simple.
from the scene
I found it, restarted the server, and finished alone.
from your own week
She read it, signed it, and sent it back.
Then I found it, restarted the server, and finished alone.

Who it sounds like acted

The idea
This part is your choice. The thing you put in the past continuous is the one that was already running. The person in the past continuous sounds less like the cause. Two true sentences about one event can put the blame in two places.
in the continuous
We were running the final test when the server crashed.
in the past simple
We ran the final test and the server crashed.
Same two facts. The client reads two different stories.

Watch out

Some verbs never take -ing. Some verbs are not actions. Know, want, need, believe and understand are simply true or not true. They have no -ing form. Say “I knew the answer,” never “I was knowing the answer.”

The was or were is not optional. Say “While we were testing,” never “While we testing.” Your own language can put this tense inside the verb. English needs the two words together, every time.

The moves

The frame stays the same. You change only the blue words. The column you pick decides who the reader looks at.

The action is underway

  • We were [running] the test when it [crashed].
  • While I was [checking] it, the client [left].
  • They were [waiting] when I [arrived].

The action is complete

  • We [ran] the test and it [crashed].
  • I [checked] it and the client [left].
  • I [arrived] and they [were] still there.

The band was already running. The red line is the moment the second action interrupted it.

Why this works

A person listening to you cannot look at two things at once. So English lets you present one action as the situation and the other as the event. The one in the past continuous is the situation. The one on top is what happened. Your listener never has to guess which is which, because the verb already told him. And once you can move an action from the top to the bottom, you are not only saying what happened. You are choosing what the other person notices first. That is most of what telling a story is.

ACTIVATION

Goal. Now practice. Build the two tenses. Fix what English does not allow. Join two actions. Choose what your reader sees. Then write sentences of your own.

A

Simple or continuous?

Put each verb into the past simple or the past continuous.

B

Spot and fix the error

Each sentence has one mistake. Write it correctly.

C

Combine the actions

Join the two actions into one sentence. Put the action that was already running in the past continuous. The order on the page is not the answer. Where an item tells you how to start, start there, and remember the comma.

D

Choose the framing

Both sentences are correct English. Look at who is going to read it, and choose.

E

Vocabulary: telling a story

Match each expression to its meaning.

F

Write a sentence

Write ONE sentence with each expression. Put two actions in it: one that was already running, and one that interrupts it.

This check looks at three things only: that your expression is there, that the tense is there, and that it looks like a sentence. It cannot tell you whether what you wrote is true or natural. The model is there for that, and you are the one who compares.

G

Over to you

No checking here. This one is yours.

In pairs. Tell the story of a time something went wrong at work. Use the past continuous for the thing that was already happening, and the past simple for what happened: “I was giving a presentation when the projector died. While I was looking for a cable, the room went quiet.” Then tell it a second time, moving yourself out from underneath. Ask your partner which version sounds more like your fault.

On your own. Write four or five sentences about a day that did not go to plan. Use at least three past continuous verbs and three past simple verbs, and join some of them with when and while. Read it aloud until it flows.
