9 · OFF: separation, departure, and cancellation

Ending things, leaving rooms, and taking plans off the calendar.

ACCESS

Read the scene and notice the moves in bold. One of them, peel off, is never taught in this book. Before you read on, let the particle show you what it means.

A law firm's deal team, the night before a signing that may not happen.

Nadia The seller wants to call off tomorrow's signing until the financing clears.

Theo If we put it off a third time, the buyer walks.

Ruiz And if the talks break off tonight, there's nothing left to sign at all.

Nadia Then peel off the disputed warranties into a side letter and sign the core deal on time.

Theo Their lead counsel already took off for the night. We can't reach him.

Nadia Then head off to his office at eight, before he sets off for the airport. We settle this in person.

ANALYSIS

Off is the mirror of on, and its hub is just as physical: away from a surface. Take a lid off a jar. A plane lifts off the runway. Every abstract sense in this unit is that one separation applied to something you cannot literally peel.

In the separation sense, a part detaches from a whole: talks break off, a supply is cut off. In the departure sense, something leaves under its own power: people take off, head off, set off. In the cancellation sense, a plan is lifted off the calendar: you call off an event, or back down from a position.

The hub is the literal sense. Each spoke is a step away from it. This unit takes the three solid spokes; Unit 10 takes the rest.

Watch out. Put off means two different things. "The meeting was put off" means it was postponed. "His tone put me off" means it repelled me. And break off is not break down: break off is a deliberate, abrupt end; break down is a failure or collapse.
American / British English. American speech often adds "of": "get off of the ladder." British English, and careful American writing, drop it: "get off the ladder." This book uses off without of.

Hold "off means away from the surface," and the only question is what comes away. A part breaks off, a person takes off, or a plan is lifted off the calendar. This is also why call off feels final and put off feels temporary: one lifts the event off the calendar for good; the other slides it to a later square.

hive off: to separate a part of a business into its own entity. Separation spoke.

run off: to leave hurriedly. Departure spoke.

stave off: to hold a threat or a deadline away for a while. Cancellation spoke, with the sense of keeping it off.
