22 · INTO: transition, investigation, pressure, and collision

Becoming, examining, persuading, and colliding.

ACCESS

Read the scene and notice the moves in bold. One of them, wade into, is never taught in this book. Before you read on, let the particle show you what it means.

An internal audit team, the morning a routine review starts to look like something more.

Nadia The expense flags turned into something bigger. We need to look into the whole vendor list.

Sam I'll dig into the payments over fifty thousand first. That's where the pattern is.

Nadia Good. But don't get talked into narrowing the scope. Finance will push for that.

Sam Understood. I already waded into the reconciliations. Half the receipts are missing.

Nadia Then we delve into the approvals too. Someone signed these off.

Sam Agreed. I ran into the controller this morning. He got defensive the second I mentioned it.

ANALYSIS

Into's hub is physical: crossing a boundary inward. Walk into a room, pour water into a glass. Every abstract sense in this unit is that crossing applied to becoming, examining, pressuring, or colliding.

In the transition sense, one thing crosses over and becomes another: turn into, grow into. In the investigation sense, you go inside a matter to examine it: look into, dig into. In the pressure sense, you move someone into a decision they resisted: talk into, push into. In the collision sense, you cross paths with something and hit it: run into, bump into.

The hub is the literal sense. Each spoke is a way of crossing inward. All four belong to this unit.

Watch out. Run into has several senses: meet a person by chance, hit an obstacle, or reach a number (costs ran into the millions). And talk into is the opposite of talk out of: persuade to do it, versus persuade not to.
American / British English. Both varieties run into someone by chance. American English also says run across for the same; British English uses come across. This book uses run into for people and come across for things found by chance.

Hold into means across the boundary to the inside, and the only question is what crosses: one thing becomes another, you enter a matter to examine it, a person is moved into a choice, or you cross paths and collide. This is why look into a matter and run into a wall use the same particle: both cross a line, one on purpose to examine, one by accident to a stop.

tap into: to gain access to a resource or supply. Investigation spoke.

coax into: to gently persuade someone into doing something. Pressure spoke.
