Where the number landed

The update was clear. It just never said how far behind.

The Reading. Three minutes on the English you actually use at work, every Tuesday. Leave any time.

01 IN THE WILD · SLACK  

Thursday afternoon. Three people in the channel, two of them in another country.

quick one on the billing rollout. retention’s holding, resolution times way down. adoption’s a bit behind but nothing scary. happy to jump on a call if useful

Nobody replies. On Monday someone asks why billing is in trouble.
02 THE GAUGE  
         
FROZEN FORMAL CONSULTATIVE CASUAL INTIMATE

Needle: casual. The register is right and it is not the problem. Nothing here is measured.

Casual is not the same as vague. Where did we land is informal and asks for a number. A bit behind is informal and names nothing. Same register, one of them works.

NOT THIS

adoption’s a bit behind but nothing scary

 

SAY THIS

adoption’s at 61, we wanted 70. that’s the one to watch

When it has to travel up, a director in the thread or a record someone reads in six months, the same fact goes formal: adoption came in 9 points short of target. Same number, different room.

The Italian trap. Rispondere alle aspettative pushes you toward respond to the expectations. English meets them: it met expectations. But look at what that buys you. Met expectations is as unmeasured as a bit behind. The grammar gets fixed and the sentence still says nothing.
03 ONE REP  

In your next message in a channel, find where you gave a number without saying what it was measured against. Add the target.

Then watch whether anyone asks a follow-up question. If they do, your old wording was carrying nothing.
04 THE KIT  
come in Where a number landed. Retention came in at 71 percent. The particle carries it; come alone says nothing.
fall short of Softer than miss, and it invites a reason. Miss closes the topic. Fall short of opens it.
nothing scary Closes a subject before anyone opens it. Honest when there is nothing to watch, and worth saying then. Over a number you have not named it reads as do not ask, and nobody does.
shake out How something ends once the noise settles. Casual. Fine on a call, risky in a doc that a director will read.
land Where did we land? Very common in American meetings. It asks for a summary without demanding one.
a bit The vaguest quantifier in the language. Fine in a bit late. In a bit behind it is standing where the number should be.
05 WHY IT STICKS · PSYCHOLINGUISTICS  

You do not build came in ahead of plan one word at a time. A fluent speaker pulls it out whole, as one stored piece, which is why it arrives fast and why the preposition never wanders. You are assembling it from parts, and assembly costs working memory.

Under pressure, in a meeting, in your second language, the parts are what you drop first. That is why this error shows up in your speech and not in your writing. Writing gave you time.

So do not drill the preposition. Say the whole thing out loud until it comes in one breath: came in ahead of plan, came in short of target, in line with the forecast. Three chunks, not nine words. What you retrieve, you keep. What you assemble, you lose.
