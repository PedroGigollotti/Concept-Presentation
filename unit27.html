27 · COME and GO across the particles

Arrival, outcome, and failure: toward you, and away.

ACCESS

A clinic with two verbs, and they are opposites. Come moves toward you; go moves away. One phrase, go about, is never taught here. Predict it from the particle before you read on.

A startup founder and lead, the day after a big demo, working out what the result really means.

Priya The demo went well, but one thing keeps coming up: the price.

Sam Right. We come across as premium, but the market wants cheaper.

Priya So it comes down to positioning. Are we the premium option or not?

Sam Depends how the new tier goes over with existing customers.

Priya If churn spikes, the whole model could go under. Let's not pretend otherwise.

Sam Fair. So how do we go about the repricing without spooking everyone?

ANALYSIS

ONE QUESTION RUNS THIS WHOLE UNIT

Toward me, or away from me?

Two verbs, pulling opposite ways. Come moves toward the speaker and is about arrival and emergence; go moves away and is about outcome and departure. Fix the direction first and the particle is easier than it looks.

The structure

This clinic runs two verbs at once, and they pull in opposite directions. Come moves toward you; go moves away.

Come is about arrival and emergence. A topic comes up (rises into view), you come across a certain way (arrive at an impression), a decision comes down to one thing (settles to its base). Go is about outcome and departure. It goes over well (lands with an audience), it goes through (passes all the way), or it goes under (sinks and fails).

Two verbs, opposite directions. Come brings it toward you; go sends it away. Read down each table and predict before the meaning column.

Come across, and get across

One of the most useful pairs in the book, and the difference is exactly the difference between the two verbs.

Come across is about you: it is the impression that arrives at the listener, whether you intended it or not. Get across, from Unit 23, is about your message: it is the content arriving, because you worked to send it.

So a presentation can get the risk across perfectly and still come across as defensive. Both crossed the same gap. Only one of them was the thing you were trying to send.

The test you can run with no book

Ask: which way is it moving relative to you?

Toward you and the verb is come: something arrives, appears, or reaches you. Away from you and it is go: something departs, lands elsewhere, or sinks.

Then read the particle exactly as its own unit taught it. Up is into view, across is over to a receiver, over is across and above, under is below the surface.

The mechanical half: come across takes as. He comes across as certain. Without as, come across means to find something by chance instead: I came across the old file.

Watch out. Go over has two senses: be received well by people, or review something (go over the numbers). And come across, come up, come out are three different arrivals: make an impression or find by chance, arise as a topic, or be revealed.
American / British English. British English says the file went missing. American English uses it too now, but also says the file is missing or disappeared. This book uses go missing.

Two opposite verbs, one set of particles, and the particles behave exactly as they always do. Come up and go under are miles apart, but not because of anything mysterious. Come rises and arrives; go departs and, with under, sinks. The verbs set the direction; the particles set the fate.

Your prediction: go about. You predicted this one from about before you read any of the unit: you take the task from its sides: the manner of your approach, not the fact of starting. Across this unit go is movement and departure, and the particle says how.
