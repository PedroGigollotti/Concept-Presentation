ANALYSIS

Goal. Learn to turn an action into a noun when the register needs it, and to know what you gave up when you did.

ONE QUESTION RUNS THIS WHOLE UNIT

Does the sentence still say who did it?

If it does not, decide whether that is what you meant. Nominalization removes the actor, and a reader notices when it removes them at a convenient moment.

THE FORM

Verb to nounverb + suffix such as -ion, -ment, -al, -ance
Adjective to nounadjective + suffix such as -ness, -ity, -anceOur awareness of the risk had grown significantly. Watch out Don't over-nominalize until the sentence loses its actor. The implementation of the decision regarding the reduction of headcount... stacks nominalizations until nobody is doing anything. Keep at least one clear verb and a visible subject.

The verb becomes a noun and the actor disappears

We decided to delay becomes the decision to delay. The action survives; the person does not.

That is the whole trade. What you gain is a phrase you can put a verb around: the decision to delay was made after careful review, the decision stands, the decision predates the audit.

What you lose is the actor, and in a compliance context that loss is sometimes the point and sometimes a problem. The form does not tell you which; you do.

The test you can run with no book

Read the sentence and ask: does it still say who did it?

If not, ask a second question: did I want it to? A summary to a board may legitimately not name the individual. A report on a failure usually should.

The mechanical half: find the noun that has a verb inside it - decision, implementation, recognition, awareness - and try saying the sentence with the verb instead. If the verb version needs a subject you cannot supply, the nominalization was hiding a gap rather than a person.

Stacking is where it stops being English

One nominalization per clause reads as formal. Three read as unparsable.

“The implementation of the decision regarding the reduction of headcount” has four nouns doing the work of two verbs, and the reader has to hold all four before anything happens.

The repair is always the same: pick the one action that matters and give it back its verb. We decided to reduce headcount, and implementing that took longer than expected.

What the register buys you

The compliance officer is not hiding. She is presenting decisions as objects the board can examine, which is what a board meeting is for.

There's growing recognition of the need for further review says that several people, unnamed, now think the same thing. A verb version would force her to say who, and the point is that it is no longer one person's view.

The judgment: use it when the thing, not the doer, is what is under discussion. Reach back for the verb the moment somebody needs to act.

Watch out

Don't over-nominalize until the sentence loses its actor. “The implementation of the decision regarding the reduction of headcount” stacks four nouns where two verbs would do. Pick the action that matters and give it back its verb.

Check whether the missing actor is a choice. The decision was made is legitimate in a summary and evasive in a failure report. The grammar is the same; the judgment is yours.

The moves

Same shift from action to concept in every line. The highlighted slot changes; the noun form always carries the formal weight.

Presenting it as a thing

  • The decision to [delay / pause the filing] was made after careful review.
  • The implementation of the [policy / new process] took longer than expected.
  • There's growing recognition of the need for [further review / an external audit].

Giving the verb back

  • We [decided to delay / chose to pause] after reviewing it carefully.
  • We became much more aware of the [risk / exposure].
  • It took us longer than expected to [implement the policy / roll the process out].

An everyday verb sits on the left. The same idea, nominalized for a formal report, sits on the right.

Why this works

Board papers, filings and policy documents are written about decisions rather than about the people who made them, and nominalization is the grammar that makes that possible: it turns an act into an object you can date, compare and question. The same move is how bad news gets written without an author, so a reader at this level is always checking whether the missing actor was a matter of register or a matter of convenience. Knowing what you removed is what lets you defend having removed it.
