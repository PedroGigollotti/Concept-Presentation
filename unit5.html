5 · UP: completion and finality

Closing out a project.

ACCESS

Read the scene and notice the moves in bold. One of them, true up, is never taught in this book. Before you read on, let the particle show you what it means.

A clinical research team, three days from closing a study site.

Ruth We need to wrap up the site by Friday. Where does everything stand?

Dev Data entry is nearly there. About forty queries left to clear up.

Amara I'll tie up the consent files. Two of them are missing a signature.

Ruth Good. And we've used up almost the whole monitoring budget, so no extra visits.

Dev The lab still hasn't invoiced us for the final batch.

Amara I'll square up with them Monday, once the last shipment lands.

Ruth Then let's finish up and send the closeout letter.

ANALYSIS

Up begins where every child learns it: physical motion toward the top. Hands up. Stand up. But English does something patterned with that image, and once you see the step, much of the particle stops looking arbitrary.

If you move up, you eventually arrive at a top, and a top is a limit. Reach the limit of a task and the task is finished. Reach the limit of a supply and the supply is gone. Reach the limit of what you owe and the account is closed. Height, then limit, then finish: one short chain, and it explains why three senses that feel unrelated all take one particle.

The literal sense sits at the top. Everything under it is that same movement applied to something you cannot touch.

Watch out. Put a pronoun before the particle. Say “I'll use it up,” never “I'll use up it.” With a full noun phrase either order works. And do not add up to a verb that already finishes: finalize up the contract is wrong.
American / British English. Wash up: American, to wash your hands and face; British, to do the dishes. This book uses the American sense throughout.

Your working memory holds about four things at once. Store “up means reaching the limit,” and wrap up, use up, and settle up stop being three items. They are one idea applied to three limits: a task, a supply, a debt.

Meet a Stranger

These three are not taught anywhere in this book. Reason from the particle, then reveal the key.

1. “Finance asked us to true up the forecast against actual spend before quarter end.”

2. “Let's wind up here. We're already five minutes over.”

3. “Legal thinks we can sew up the contract by Thursday.”

true up: to adjust figures so they match reality. Completion sense.

wind up: to bring a meeting or an event to its end. Completion sense, close to wrap up.

sew up: to finalize and secure something so it cannot come undone. Completion sense.
