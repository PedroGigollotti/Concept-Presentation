12 · Mixed conditionals

The grammar of hindsight.

ANALYSIS

Goal. Learn how the structure is built, what it means, and how to shift its tone. By the end you should be able to explain it and control who it blames.

The structure

A mixed conditional connects two different unreal times in one sentence. An ordinary conditional keeps both halves in the same time; a mixed conditional deliberately breaks that symmetry. That is why it is the natural grammar of hindsight: the cause sits in a past that did not happen, and the consequence sits in a present that therefore is not true.

Past to present

Form
if + past perfect, would + base verb
Meaning
a past action that did not happen, so a present that is not true
Use it to
show how an earlier decision still shapes the present
If we had locked the scope, we wouldn’t be slipping now.

Present to past

Form
if + past simple or were, would have + past participle
Meaning
a present condition that is not true, so a past that came out differently
Use it to
explain a past event by a present trait or standing fact
If I were more cautious, I wouldn’t have signed it.

Watch out

Do not collapse the two times. "If we locked it, we won't slip" mixes the tenses wrongly and marks you as non-native at once.

Keep "would" out of the if-clause. Write "if we had locked," never "if we would have locked."

American / British English.

Subjunctive "were." American English keeps "if I were" in every person; informal British English often allows "if I was." This book uses "were."

Past participle of "get." American "we would have gotten it done"; British "we would have got it done." This book uses "gotten."

The moves

Same grammar in every line. Only the highlighted slot and the subject change.

  • If you'd flagged [it / the risk], we wouldn't be here.
  • If I'd [spoken up / pushed], we might not be in this position.
  • If we'd caught it earlier, we'd be ahead now.
  • Things would look different now if [the brief / the spec] had been clearer.
  • Had [the timeline / the plan] been realistic, we wouldn't be scrambling.

Where each move sits

The grammar is identical. What changes is who the sentence blames: you, me, us, a thing, or no one.

Why this works

A mixed conditional makes you hold two unreal times at once: a past that did not happen and the present that therefore does not exist. That is a heavy load, and under pressure most people collapse it into one time and say "if we locked it, we won't slip," which is wrong and sounds wrong. Store the frame whole, "if we had X, we wouldn't be Y," and your attention is free for the harder choice: who the sentence blames. The grammar never changes, yet the small shift from "you" to "we" to an agentless "had the timeline been..." moves the needle from fault to no fault. In a retro, that choice decides whether the team fixes the problem or goes quiet.
