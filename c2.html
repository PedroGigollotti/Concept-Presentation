Professional English Practice: C2

Twenty-two units. Study the Access and Analysis sections, then work through the interactive exercises. Search for the grammar you need, or work straight through.

This is Book 4 of Professional English Practice, calibrated to C2: the mastery layer for professionals who already command C1 accuracy and are ready for nuance, style, and register control. Book 4 revisits a portion of the C1 canon at real depth, alongside structures that barely appear before this level, and adds the one competence that defines C2 itself: saying the same thing three different ways, on purpose, for three different audiences.

Twenty-two units carry you from aspect and formal conditionals through modality, marked word order, cohesion, and reporting, ending in the block that defines the level: reformulation, figurative control, connotation, and full-range register. Each one stands on its own, so you can work the book start to finish or open directly to the topic in front of you this week.

Each unit follows the same cycle, so the shape of the book never surprises you once you know it.

Access. Read a short, authentic workplace scene. The target grammar is already there, highlighted in bold. You are noticing the pattern, not producing it yet.

Analysis. Learn exactly how the structure is built, what it means, and how shifting it changes your tone. This section ends by showing you where the grammar sits on a visual scale or timeline, so the idea has a shape, not just a rule.

Activation. A full bank of exercises, moving from mechanical accuracy through tone and vocabulary to real, personal production.

Over to you. The last section of every unit, and the reason the rest of it exists. It runs in three steps. Under pressure: four workplace situations, one clock each, thirty to sixty seconds depending on how much judgment the unit asks of you. Say your line out loud, then type it. The model appears when the clock runs out and not before, so there is nothing to gain by waiting. This is deliberate: you already understand the grammar by this point, and what you are building here is the ability to reach for it in the second you need it, which only comes from retrieving it against a clock. Say it: record one line, play it back, and listen for the one thing the unit tells you to listen for. Tone lives in the voice before it lives on the page, so a book that teaches register in silence is describing a sound you never hear. With a partner, if you have one: the paired role-play and the written task. Everything before it works alone.

Answer Key. Every exercise, worked in full. In this interactive edition the key is built into each exercise, so press Check my answers and the correct form appears beside anything you missed. The timed situations and the recording are not scored, because production is not a right-or-wrong item; there the clock, the model, and your own voice are the feedback.

Two ways to work through it. Start to finish, for a full learning path through the grammar of professional English. Or unit by unit, jumping straight to whatever you need this week using the search field below.

Do not skip the final section. Do the timed situations and the recording in every unit, and the pair work whenever you have someone to do it with. That is where the grammar stops being a rule you know and becomes a sentence you actually say. Everything before it is preparation for it.

I started The Concept Method because I kept meeting brilliant professionals who could read a technical spec in English without blinking, and then freeze in a retro when they needed to disagree with their manager gently, or soften a piece of bad news, or make a joke land in a language that was not the one they grew up thinking in.

That gap is not a knowledge problem. It is a grammar problem hiding as a confidence problem. The structures in this book, the inverted conditional you use to assign blame kindly in a postmortem, the hedge that lets you say something true without overpromising, the cleft sentence that puts the one fact that matters at the front of the room, are not academic curiosities. They are the actual tools fluent speakers reach for without noticing, every single day, under real pressure.

So that is what this book teaches: not English for its own sake, but English for the Tuesday afternoon meeting where it actually counts. Every scene in this book is something that could happen at your job this week, because it is exactly the kind of moment this method was built for.

Mind your future. It is closer than you think, and it is going to need you to say the right thing at the right time.

Pedro Gigollotti NettoFounder, The Concept Method
