1 · Verb plus particle: one idea

Noticing them, and why stand up and bring up are the same machine.

ACCESS

Welcome. Before any particle, one idea: a phrasal verb is a verb and a particle acting as a single unit, with a single meaning. You already use dozens. This scene is full of them. One, ping back, you may not have met. Guess it from the particle.

A morning standup. Count the phrasal verbs as they go by; they're everywhere in ordinary speech.

Mara Let's kick off. Sol, where did you get to yesterday?

Sol I set up the staging server and looked into the login bug.

Mara Did you figure it out?

Sol Mostly. I'll follow up with a fix and ping back when it's live.

Mara Great. Priya, anything holding you up?

Priya Just waiting on design. I'll chase it up after the standup.

ANALYSIS

A phrasal verb is a verb plus a particle, a small word like up, out, off, or in, that together act as one unit with one meaning. Look and into are two words; look into is one idea: investigate. The particle isn't a preposition doing its own job here; it has fused with the verb.

The remarkable part is that the same machine runs from the most literal to the most figurative. Stand up can mean rise from your chair. Add to and it becomes resist: stand up to the boss. Bring up can mean carry something upstairs, or raise a topic. Verb plus particle, one machine, sliding from the concrete to the abstract.

The same verb-plus-particle machine, from lifting a real box to facing down real power. Only the height on the ladder changes.

Watch out. Not every verb-plus-word is a phrasal verb. In she ran up the hill, up is a preposition of direction. In she ran up a bill, up is a particle: accumulated. Test: can you move the object? She ran a bill up works; she ran the hill up does not. The movable one is the phrasal verb.
American / British English. British English rings someone up; American English calls someone up, or just calls. Same machine, different verb. This book uses call.

Seeing the phrasal verb as one unit is the shift that unlocks the rest of the book. Read look into as one verb meaning investigate, and it's as simple as any other word.

Meet a Stranger

These three are not taught anywhere in this book. Reason from the particle, then reveal the key. No grading here, this is reasoning practice.

1. “I'll hash out the details before the client call.”

2. “Let's chase down the last two approvals before the call.”

3. “Let's knuckle down and finish the sprint.”

hash out: to work through a problem by discussing it thoroughly. Out: fully worked through.

chase down: to pursue and finally obtain something. Down: pursued until caught.

knuckle down: to begin working hard and seriously. Down: settle low and focus.
