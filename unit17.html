17 · BACK: return, reversal, restraint, and resistance

Coming back, undoing, holding in reserve, and pushing back.

ACCESS

Read the scene and notice the moves in bold. One of them, walk back, is never taught in this book. Before you read on, let the particle show you what it means.

A city agency, days after a fee increase set off a public firestorm.

Lena The fee increase triggered a firestorm. Comms wants us to walk back the whole thing.

Omar We shouldn't roll back the entire policy. Let's scale back the increase and keep the core.

Lena Agreed. Hold back the press release until the council briefing tomorrow.

Omar The opposition will push back hard no matter what we announce.

Lena Let them. I'll get back to the council chair tonight with the revised numbers.

Omar Good. I'll report back after the community meeting. We keep some contingency pulled back for now.

ANALYSIS

Back's hub is simple and physical: toward the point of origin. Step back, go back home, look back. Every abstract sense in this unit is a return to an earlier point, whether in place, in time, in reserve, or in an exchange.

In the return sense, you come back to a person or a task: get back to someone, report back. In the reversal sense, you move a situation back toward an earlier state: roll back a policy, walk back a claim. In the restraint sense, you hold something back at the line: hold back, pull back. In the resistance sense, you send force back the way it came: push back, hit back.

The hub is the literal sense. Each spoke is a way of pointing back. All four belong to this unit; back doesn't split across two units the way UP, OUT, OFF, ON, IN, and DOWN do.

Watch out. Roll back is not walk back. Roll back undoes a policy or a change. Walk back softens a statement you made. And answer back means reply rudely or defiantly, usually to someone in authority, not just "reply."
American / British English. Both varieties use get back to someone for replying later. British English also says "come back to you on that." This book uses get back to.

Hold "back means toward where it started," and the only question is what returns: a reply, a policy, a resource held in reserve, or a shove sent back the way it came. This is why push back feels like a fight and get back to you feels like a courtesy, though they share a particle.
