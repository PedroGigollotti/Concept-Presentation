25 · PUT across the particles

Positioning and deferring: where a thing goes, and when.

ACCESS

Another prediction clinic. The verb put never changes; only the particle moves. One phrase, put out, is used here in a sense we never teach. Predict it from the particle before you read on.

An operations team preparing a board proposal while the clock and a slow vendor work against them.

Marcus The board wants the proposal Friday. Let's not put off the pricing decision any longer.

Lena Agreed. I'll put forward the phased rollout; it's the safest option to defend.

Marcus Good. Put in a risk section too. The board always asks.

Lena Will do. Honestly, I've put up with the vendor's delays long enough. We need a backup.

Marcus Fair. I don't want to put you out, but can you also handle the deck?

Lena I've got it. The key is putting the numbers across clearly, or the message gets lost.

ANALYSIS

Same clinic move as the last unit. The verb put holds perfectly still, so whatever shifts, from postponing to proposing to enduring, comes from the particle alone. Put is pure placement; it has no color of its own.

Because put is about position, its particles are about where and when. Put it off (away in time, postpone), put it forward (ahead, propose), put it in (inside, submit), put up with it (hold it up and bear it), put it down (onto the page, or onto someone), put it through (along a channel, connect), put it across (over to a receiver, communicate).

Read down the particle column. You already know each one. Predict the meaning before your eye reaches the third column.

Watch out. Put off has two senses: postpone a meeting, or repel a person. And put down has many senses: pay a deposit, euthanize a pet, belittle someone, suppress a rebellion, or record something in writing. The object decides every time.
American / British English. Both varieties put a call through (connect it). British English also puts someone through university (pays for it); American English says put someone through school. This book uses put through school.

Put is the purest test of the method, because it is nothing but placement and has no color of its own. So when put off means postpone and put across means communicate, you know for certain the difference is the particle, because there is nothing else it could be.

put by — to save something for later use. By: set in reserve, off to the side.
