10 · OFF: triggering, using up, and finishing

Firing things into motion, using them down to nothing, and finishing them clean.

ACCESS

Read the scene and notice the moves in bold. One of them, cap off, is never taught in this book. Before you read on, let the particle show you what it means.

A construction site team at a milestone meeting, deciding what starts and what finishes this week.

Dana We kick off the foundation pour Monday, weather permitting.

Owen Good. If we hold the date, the completion bonus pays off the equipment loan by June.

Priya One risk. The road closure could set off complaints from the shops next door.

Dana Let's work off the punch list this week so the inspector can sign off on the pour by Friday.

Dana Agreed. Round off the figures for the update and finish off the safety report tonight.

ANALYSIS

Off is the same hub you met in Unit 9: away from a surface. That unit used it for things separating, leaving, or being canceled. This one takes the other edge of the same movement: the point where something breaks loose into action, gets consumed all the way down, or has its last piece taken off and is done.

In the triggering sense, a charge releases: you set off an alarm, kick off an event. In the using-up sense, something is spent all the way to nothing: you pay off a debt, work off a backlog. In the finishing sense, the last edge is smoothed and the thing is complete: you round off, finish off.

Nothing about the particle changed since Unit 9. Only which half of its map this unit works on.

Watch out. Pay off has two senses. Pay off a debt means discharge it. But the effort paid off means it yielded a return. And sign off needs on for approval: sign off on the report means approve it; without on, it can just mean finish writing it.
American / British English. Americans sign off on something to approve it. British English also uses signed off to mean certified unfit for work: he was signed off sick. This book uses sign off on for approval.

Hold “off means all the way off, released or gone,” and the only question is what happens at that point. Something fires, something is used up, or the last piece is taken off and it is done. This is why kick off feels like a beginning and finish off feels like an end, though they share a particle.
