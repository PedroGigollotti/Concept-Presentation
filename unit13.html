13 · IN: enclosure, inclusion, and participation

Drawing things inside, adding them to a plan, and stepping into the room.

ACCESS

Read the scene and notice the moves in bold. One of them, rope in, is never taught in this book. Before you read on, let the particle show you what it means.

A university program committee, planning a new data course and who should shape it.

Amara Before we design the course, let's bring in the departments it touches.

Ken Agreed. We should factor in the lab scheduling constraints early, or we'll redesign twice.

Amara And build in an exit survey from day one, not bolted on later.

Priya Can I sit in on the faculty workshop? I want to take in their objections firsthand.

Ken Of course. We also roped in the librarians to pilot the research module.

Amara Good. When the draft is ready, everyone weighs in before it goes to the dean.

ANALYSIS

In is the mirror of out, and its hub is just as physical: inside a boundary. Step in a room. Put a letter in an envelope. Every abstract sense in this unit is that crossing applied to a plan, a group, or a conversation.

In the enclosure sense, something is drawn inside and held: you fold an ingredient in, take a fact in. In the inclusion sense, something new is added to a plan or a group: you bring in a team, factor in a cost. In the participation sense, a person steps into a discussion: you weigh in with an opinion, sit in on a meeting.

The hub is the literal sense. Each spoke is a step away from it. This unit takes the three solid spokes; Unit 14 takes the rest.

Watch out. Take in has several senses: understand a fact, attend a show, or shelter or deceive a person, depending on context. And sit in is not sit in on: sit in on means to observe a meeting you are not running.

Hold "in means across the line to the inside," and the only question is what crosses. A fact is drawn in and held, a person or cost is added to the plan, or a voice steps into the room. This is why bring in feels like an invitation and box in feels like a trap, though they share a particle.
