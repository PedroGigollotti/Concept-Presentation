ANALYSIS

ONE QUESTION RUNS THIS WHOLE UNIT

What is being fixed in place?

Unit 15 lowered things. This unit takes the other half of down: the ground as the place where things become fixed — written onto the page, pinned so they cannot move, or come to rest after motion.

The structure

Down is the same hub you met in Unit 15: toward the ground. This one takes the senses where down means fixed to the ground: written onto the page, pinned in place, or come to rest.

In the recording sense, a fleeting thing is fixed onto paper: write it down, note it down. In the fixing sense, a loose thing is fastened so it cannot move: nail down the details, pin down a time. In the settling sense, motion slows and comes to rest: settle down, calm down, wind down.

Nothing about the particle changed since Unit 15. Only which half of its map this unit works on.

Written down, and pinned down

The first two senses look alike and do different work, and the difference is what existed before the verb.

Write down a number that was said aloud and the number was already definite — you moved it to paper. Pin down a delivery window and the window was not definite at all — you made it so, and the paper is beside the point.

So recording changes the medium and fixing changes the certainty. A meeting can end with everything written down and nothing nailed down, which is a real thing that happens and a useful sentence to be able to say.

The test you can run with no book

Ask: was it already definite before?

If it was, and you moved it onto paper, that is recording. If it was vague, and now it is not, that is fixing. If nothing was made definite and something simply stopped moving, that is settling.

Settling gives itself away in the grammar as well: it takes no object. They settled down, things wound down, the team hunkered down.

The mechanical half: fixing takes a detail, not a thing. You nail down a date, a number, a scope. You do not nail down a project.

Watch out. Take down has opposite-feeling senses: record a statement, dismantle a stage, or defeat someone, depending on the object. And pin down and nail down overlap but aren't identical: pin down leans toward a slippery person or vague thing; nail down leans toward details and plans.
American / British English. British English often drops the particle and just says note it. American English keeps note it down or jot it down. This book uses note down.

Hold “down means fixed to the ground,” and the only question is what gets fixed: a fleeting fact onto the page, a loose detail into place, or a racing team to a stop. This is why note it down feels light and lock it down feels final, though they share a particle.

Your prediction: hunker down. You predicted this one from down before you read any of the unit: they settle into one place and stay there until the work is done. That is the settling sense, and the particle is what told you.
