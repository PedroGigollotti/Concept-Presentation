11 · ON: contact, continuation, and undertaking

Keeping hold, keeping moving, and taking the work on.

ACCESS

Read the scene and notice the moves in bold. One of them, soldier on, is never taught in this book. Before you read on, let the particle show you what it means.

A logistics operation at peak season, the morning standup on the busiest week of the year.

Mara We're at record volume. Can the night shift carry on at this pace through Friday?

Sol They can, but the packing line is the bottleneck. We should work on that first.

Mara Agreed. Let's not take on the returns backlog until the outbound settles.

Sol The new hires latched on fast. They're already quicker than last peak.

Mara Good. If the system lags again, hold on and reroute manually. Don't stop the line.

Sol Understood. The crew will soldier on. Let's move on to the staffing plan for Saturday.

ANALYSIS

On is the mirror of off, and its hub is just as physical: in contact with a surface. Put a cup on the table, a hand on a rail, a coat on your back. Every abstract sense in this unit is that contact held over time, or taken up as a task.

In the contact sense, something makes or keeps its grip: you put on a role, latch on to an idea, hold on to a line. In the continuation sense, the contact keeps moving along a surface: carry on, move on, press on. In the undertaking sense, you take a task onto yourself and stay on it: take on, work on, get on with.

The literal sense sits at the top. Everything under it is that same movement applied to something you cannot touch.

Watch out. Hold on has two jobs. Hold on means wait a moment. Hold on to means keep gripping or keep possession. And carry on is not carry out: carry on means continue; carry out means perform or complete a task.
American / British English. Americans do something on the weekend; the British do it at the weekend. This book uses on the weekend.

Hold on means still in contact, and the only question is what the contact is doing. It grabs and grips, it keeps rolling forward, or it takes up a task and stays on it. This is why carry on and take on feel so different though they share a particle: one keeps an action rolling, the other loads a new task onto your plate.
