7 · OUT: exit, removal, and exclusion

Cutting scope and letting people go.

ACCESS

Read the scene and notice the moves in bold. One of them, carve out, is never taught in this book. Before you read on, let the particle show you what it means.

A nonprofit, three weeks before the board approves next year's program budget.

Priya We're eleven percent over. Something has to come out.

Femi We could cut out the regional workshops. They're the biggest line after salaries.

Priya I'd rather not rule out the workshops yet. They're the only thing rural members get.

Ana Then we carve out a smaller version. Two regions instead of six.

Femi That still leaves out four regions, and they will notice.

Priya Better than the alternative. If we do nothing, the board shuts out the whole program.

ANALYSIS

Out is the mirror of in, and its hub is just as physical: away from an enclosure. Step out of a room. Take something out of a drawer. Every abstract sense in this unit is that one image applied to something you cannot literally walk out of.

The three senses differ only in who is doing the moving. In the exit sense, the thing leaves under its own power: people drop out, walk out, opt out. In the removal sense, someone takes it out: you cut out a line item, rule out an option. In the exclusion sense, the boundary is held against something that wanted in: a decision shuts people out, locks them out, freezes them out.

The literal sense sits at the top. Everything under it is that same movement applied to something you cannot touch.

Watch out. Leave out and leave off are not the same. Leave out means you omitted something that belonged. Leave off means you stopped, as in pick up where we left off. You left off my name is wrong.
American / British English. Sort out: British speakers use it constantly for fixing almost anything, including people. American usage is narrower and leans toward sorting out a problem or a mess. This book uses the narrower American sense.

Hold out means past the boundary, and the only question left is who moved. They walked out, you cut them out, or the door was held shut and they were shut out. That is one image with three agents, and choosing between them is a real decision, not a stylistic one.

bow out: to withdraw gracefully, on your own terms. Exit sense.

strip out: to remove something thoroughly, leaving only the essential structure. Removal sense.

ice out: to exclude someone gradually and without stating it. Exclusion sense.
