23 · The minor set: apart, together, aside, forward, across

apart, together, aside, forward, across: the small particles that carry formal weight.

ACCESS

Read the scene and notice the moves in bold. One of them, cobble together, is never taught in this book. Before you read on, let the particle show you what it means.

A leadership team at a strategy session, rebuilding a plan that just collapsed.

Marisol Last quarter's plan fell apart the moment the market shifted. We rebuild today.

Dev Then let's set aside the old roadmap and start from what we know now.

Marisol Agreed. Dev, pull the team together by ten. I want every function in the room.

Dev Done. Finance will put forward three scenarios; we choose one by end of day.

Marisol Good. Whatever we pick, the message has to get across cleanly to the whole company.

Dev We cobbled together a rough version overnight. Enough to react to, not to ship.

ANALYSIS

The big particles you have met each carry one image and stretch it across many senses. This unit is different. Here are five small particles, each with one tight, mostly literal meaning, and each with a formal weight that the everyday particles lack. You put a proposal forward in a meeting; you would not say you put it up.

Apart means into separate pieces: a plan falls apart, you take a claim apart. Together means into one whole: a team comes together, you pull people together. Aside means to the side, out of the way: set a concern aside, brush an objection aside. Forward means ahead, into view: put a proposal forward, look forward to a result. Across means from one side to the other, to a receiver: get a point across, cut across the usual lines.

One wheel, five small particles. Each spoke is a single tight meaning, not a family of senses.

Watch out. Look forward to takes a noun or an -ing verb: “I look forward to hearing from you,” never to hear. And come across has two senses: find something by chance, or make an impression (she comes across as confident).
American / British English. To bring a meeting forward is to move it earlier. American English often says move the meeting up for the same thing; British English says bring it forward. This book uses both, with the meaning stated.

These five are the quiet workhorses of formal English. They do not sprawl into many meanings the way up or out do; each holds one clear idea, which is exactly why they read as precise and professional. Apart separates, together unites, aside shelves, forward advances, across delivers.

2. “She waved aside the objections and moved on.”

3. “He put the new strategy across in three clear slides.”

pull apart: to separate or critique something piece by piece. Apart: into separate pieces.

wave aside: to dismiss something without serious attention. Aside: out of the way.

put across: to communicate an idea so it is understood. Across: to a receiver.
