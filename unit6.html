6 · UP: increase, emergence, and confrontation

Escalating and raising issues.

ACCESS

Read the scene and notice the moves in bold. One of them, crop up, is never taught in this book. Before you read on, let the particle show you what it means.

A manufacturing plant, after three shifts of missed targets on one line.

Tomas Line three has missed target three shifts running. What is going on?

Marcus A sealing fault came up on Tuesday and we've been patching it since.

Ines I should speak up here. I brought up the seal tolerance in April and nothing moved.

Tomas Then that one is mine. I'll own up to it.

Marcus Two more faults cropped up overnight, same station.

Tomas Right. We step up inspection there today, and if the numbers hold we scale up to the whole line.

ANALYSIS

Unit 5 followed one path out of the hub: go up far enough and you hit a limit, and a limit is a finish. This unit follows the other two, and they branch earlier. You do not have to arrive anywhere. You only have to rise.

Rise on a scale and there is more of you: more volume, more speed, more effort. That is the increase sense. Rise above a surface and you become visible: what was below the line is now in view. That is the emergence sense. Then the third sense follows from the second: raise an issue in a room and you have exposed it, and exposing something exposes you. That is confrontation.

The same wheel, turned. This unit takes the three solid spokes; the dotted ones were Unit 5.

Watch out. Come up and come up with are different verbs. A problem comes up on its own; a person comes up with an idea. "We came up a solution" is wrong. And speak up takes no object: either speak up about your concerns, or raise them.
American / British English. British speakers routinely flag up an issue. American speakers just flag it, with no particle. This book uses the American form.

Notice what the three senses share. Every one of them raises something: a number, a problem, or yourself. Raising a number is cheap. Raising a problem is harder. Raising yourself, which is what speak up and own up really do, is the expensive one.

Meet a Stranger

These three are not taught anywhere in this book. Reason from the particle, then reveal the key.

1. "Rumors of a second recall started to bubble up in the supplier forums."

2. "We'll need to beef up the inspection team before the audit."

3. "Someone at the back piped up and asked about overtime."

bubble up: to emerge gradually from below, before anyone official acknowledges it. Emergence spoke.

beef up: to strengthen something by adding capacity or resources. Increase spoke.

pipe up: to speak suddenly, often from the edge of a group. A lighter version of speak up.
