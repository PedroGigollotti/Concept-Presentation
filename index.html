THE PROBLEM

Fluent on paper. Hesitant in the room.

Your people passed the test. They read the contracts, they follow the calls. But in the negotiation, the board update, the difficult client conversation, they hesitate. They soften when they should hold firm, or push when they should hedge. The deal doesn't fail on grammar. It fails on tone.

That gap isn't vocabulary. It's pragmatics, register, and timing: the layer standard courses rarely teach, because it's harder to test and harder to sell. It's also the layer your senior people are judged on, in every room that matters.

THE METHOD

A method with a mechanism, not just a name.

Lexical Approach

Language is taught the way it's actually used, as chunks and collocations rather than isolated grammar. Roots, trunk, branches, leaves: every lesson grows from real context, not a word list.

Constructivism

Students reason through grammar by comparing it to their own native language, and reach conclusions themselves. A rule you derive sticks longer than one you're handed.

Psycholinguistics

Knowing the language is useless if your brain cannot retrieve it under pressure. We analyze cognitive load, working memory limits, and mother-tongue interference to build rapid automaticity. We don't just train what you say; we train how your brain accesses it when the stakes are high.
