24 · TAKE across the particles

Ownership and responsibility: hold the verb still and let the particle move.

ACCESS

This is a prediction clinic. The verb take never changes; only the particle moves, and the particle carries the meaning. One phrase, take on board, is never taught here. Predict it from the particle before you read on.

A newly promoted lead and their manager, mapping out the first week of owning a team.

Priya Congratulations. You take over the team officially Monday.

Sam Thanks. I'd like to take on the migration project as my first big push.

Priya Good. Just don't take on too much at once. Take up one priority and land it.

Sam Fair. I'll take in the team's context first, before I change anything.

Priya Smart. And when they give feedback, take it on board, even the hard parts.

Sam I will. If I get something wrong, I'll take it back and fix it. No ego.

ANALYSIS

In every unit so far, the particle moved and the verb changed with it. This clinic flips the axis. The verb take stays perfectly still, so whatever shifts, from accepting a job to leaving the ground, is coming from the particle alone.

Take is a good verb to prove it on, because it means almost nothing by itself. To take is just to get hold of something. Add a particle and a direction appears.

Particle Phrase Meaning
ontake onaccept a task, challenge, or responsibility
overtake overassume control of something
uptake upbegin or raise something; occupy time
intake inabsorb and understand, or be deceived
outtake outremove or extract; obtain formally
backtake backretract a statement, or return something
offtake offleave or depart; remove; suddenly succeed

Read down the particle column. You already know each one. Predict the meaning before your eye reaches the third column.

Watch out. Take off has three senses: leave or depart, remove, or, informally, suddenly succeed. And take in is a trap: understand, attend, shelter, or deceive, all with the same phrase. Read the object before you commit.
American / British English. British English can take a decision; American English almost always makes a decision. This book uses make a decision.

Hold the verb still and the lesson is impossible to miss: take barely means anything until a particle gives it a direction. On loads a responsibility onto you; over slides control across from someone else; in pulls something inward; out pushes it away. The verb is the hand; the particle is where the hand moves.

take down — to dismantle, or to write down. Down: to the ground, or onto the page.

take apart — to dismantle, or to criticize in detail. Apart: into separate pieces.

take aside — to draw someone away for a private word. Aside: to the side.
