# The Reading, design brief for Claude Design

Paste section A as your first message. Then use any prompt from section B to
push in a direction. Section C is for when you want to judge what comes back.

---

## A. The brief, paste this first

I need visual directions for a weekly email newsletter called The Reading. Show
me three distinct layout concepts, not variations of one.

**Who it is for.** Corporate professionals at a Milan tech company, aged 25 to
45, English at C1 and C2 level but not native. They read it on a phone at 8am
on a Tuesday, standing, before their first meeting. Three-minute read.

**What it is.** An English communication newsletter from Pedro Gigollotti, who
teaches these people in person. Teacher to student, not brand to prospect. It
teaches one expression per issue: where it sits in register, what it costs
socially, and why it survives or collapses under pressure.

**Brand: Concept.**
- Red #B3132B, near-black #0A0B0D, charcoal #1A1A1A, platinum #E7E8EA, gray #6B7075
- Georgia for display and quoted language, Arial for body and labels
- The mark is a gauge: a thin ring with a gap, a red needle pointing upper
  right, a red tip dot. It is an instrument for reading a gap, not decoration.
- Tagline: Mind Your Future
- No emojis. No em dashes. American English.

**Fixed content structure**, five sections in this order every issue:

1. IN THE WILD, a three-turn dialogue where the expression appears in context
2. THE GAUGE, analysis, including a five-point register dial showing where the
   expression sits: frozen, formal, consultative, casual, intimate
3. ONE REP, a single instruction to try in a real conversation this week
4. THE KIT, five related terms with register notes, two columns
5. WHY IT STICKS, the psycholinguistic reason, quieter closing note

There is also a SAY THIS, NOT THIS pair contrasting a learner error with the
correct sentence. It is the most screenshotted element, so treat it as a
feature, not a footnote.

**Hard constraints.** This is an HTML email, so the design must be buildable
with nested tables and inline styles only. 600 pixels wide maximum. No CSS grid,
no flexbox, no web fonts, no background images, no JavaScript. Assume half the
readers have images blocked, so the layout must work with type and background
colors alone. Any graphic element must be constructible from table cells with
background colors.

**What I want from you.** Three concepts that differ in structure, not in
color. For each, show the full letter at 600 pixels and a phone view at 375.
Tell me what each concept prioritizes and what it gives up.

---

## B. Directions to push

Use one per message after the first response.

**Editorial.** Make it look like a printed column in a serious weekly.
Generous white space, strong typographic hierarchy, minimal ornament, numbered
sections. The authority comes from restraint.

**Instrument panel.** Lean into the gauge. The register dial becomes the
organizing device rather than one element inside a section. Everything reads as
a measurement being taken.

**Field notes.** Make it feel handwritten in structure if not in typeface.
Observed language, annotated. Marginalia, rules, and callouts rather than boxes.

**Contrast-led.** Build the whole letter around the SAY THIS, NOT THIS pair.
The wrong sentence and the right sentence are the hero. Everything else supports
them.

**Card versus flow.** Show me the same content twice: once with every section in
a bordered card, once with no borders at all and only typography and space
separating sections. I want to see which reads faster.

**Dark.** The brand's book covers are near-black with red. Show me a version
where the letter is dark rather than light, and tell me honestly whether it
helps or just looks fashionable.

---

## C. Questions to ask about anything it produces

- What is the first thing my eye lands on, and is that the thing that matters?
- Does this still work with every image blocked?
- Which element would a reader screenshot and send to a colleague?
- Where does the design get boring on the second issue, and on the twelfth?
- What would break if a section ran twice as long?
- Does the register dial read as an instrument or as a progress bar?

---

## D. What must not change

- Section order and section names
- The five-point register dial and its labels
- Red used sparingly. It marks the moment of reading, it is not a theme color
- Georgia for quoted language. The reader must see language as language
- 600 pixels maximum, table-based, inline styles
- Alt text on every image, and a layout that survives image blocking
