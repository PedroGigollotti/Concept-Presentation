# THE_READING_STANDARD.md

Locked format for the weekly Concept newsletter.
Version 1.1, August 12, 2026. Visual template locked.

Governed by EMAIL_STANDARD.md for all technical and brand construction.
This document governs editorial content only.

---

## 1. What this is

**Name:** The Reading
**Audience:** Concept students at Bending Spoons and its group entities
**Cadence:** Weekly, Tuesday 08:00 Europe/Rome, never moved
**Length:** Under 550 words total, a three-minute read
**Job:** Keep students engaged with English between lessons, and produce one
small act of use per week

The letter is built on the Triple A Cycle. Access, Analysis, Activation, closing
with the psycholinguistic reason it works. It is the method in miniature, which
is what separates it from a vocabulary newsletter.

---

## 2. The five sections, fixed order, every issue

### 1. In the Wild (Access)

One piece of real English met in context, never a definition first. Two or three
sentences of setting, then the expression sitting inside it. The reader
encounters the language the way they will actually encounter it: mid-conversation,
without warning.

Settings are drawn from the professional world the readers occupy: product
reviews, written updates, integration calls, all-hands questions, client
conversations, cross-border teams. Composites only. See section 5.

### 2. The Gauge (Analysis)

Take it apart. What the expression does, where it sits on the register dial, and
where it breaks. Every entry receives a needle position: frozen, formal,
consultative, casual, or intimate.

Gen-Z and informal terms belong here, and the teaching point is never the
meaning. It is the register cost. Knowing what a term means is trivia. Knowing
what it signals about the speaker, and to whom, is the lesson.

Include L1 interference where it applies. The readership is largely Italian L1,
with Spanish, Portuguese, and other backgrounds present.

### 3. One Rep (Activation)

A single, specific thing to do in a real conversation within seven days.
Concrete enough to actually happen, small enough that it will.

Not "practice this expression." Rather: in your next written update, replace one
absolute claim with one hedged commitment, and notice whether anyone pushes back.

### 4. The Kit (Analysis, extended)

Four to six related items from the same semantic field as the week's expression.
Phrasal verbs, collocations, fixed expressions, and informal terms.

Every entry carries a register note or a cost, never a bare definition. The
teaching is not what the item means but what it signals and what it risks. A
reader should finish with options for the room, not vocabulary for a list.

At least one phrasal verb per issue where the field allows, chosen so the
particle carries the meaning. This is precisely where Italian, Portuguese, and
Spanish L1 intuition provides no help.

Placement: after One Rep, as a scannable close before the psycholinguistic note.
Item count flexes with the topic; four is the floor, six the ceiling.

### 5. Why It Sticks (Psycholinguistics)

Written student-facing, addressed as "you," structured and explanatory but never
academic. Jargon is explained in plain English immediately or not used.
Minimum three concrete examples per concept, drawn from that week's item.

Not a generic note on memory. Why *this* expression survives pressure, or why it
collapses. Working memory and cognitive load, chunking and retrieval, conceptual
metaphor, automaticity, the affective dimension. Whichever genuinely applies.

## 3. Voice

- Teacher to student. These people know Pedro personally.
- American English. Serial comma. No em dashes, no en dashes, no emojis.
- British forms flagged as contrast where useful, never as default.
- Direct, warm, unhurried. No hype, no growth-marketing register.
- Never condescending about the reader's English. They are C1 and C2
  professionals who lose the room, not beginners who lack vocabulary.
- No sentence that asks the reader to self-diagnose their level.

---

## 4. Length discipline

Under 550 words, all five sections combined. This is a hard ceiling, not a
target. The week the letter reaches 900 words is the week open rates begin
falling, and open rates do not recover once lost. If a topic will not fit, it
becomes two issues.

---

## 5. Source boundaries, non-negotiable

The readership works for a company listed on Nasdaq since July 1, 2026. This
raises the stakes on sourcing beyond ordinary professional discretion.

Never use:

- Anything a student says in a lesson, however anonymized. Students speak freely
  in lessons precisely because it is private, and forty-four colleagues might
  recognize a situation.
- Internal documents, messages, decks, or transcripts, from any source.
- Anything touching financial performance, share price, unannounced products,
  internal plans, or personnel matters. Material non-public information carries
  legal weight now, and a teacher circulating it to a company's employees is an
  indefensible position.

Always use:

- Publicly reported news, treated as context for language only.
- Realistic composites written in the register of the industry.
- The general craft of professional communication.

The test before any entry ships: is this a language lesson that happens to be
relevant, or is it commentary on their business? Only the first belongs.

## 6. Content map

Themes drawn from the linguistic demands the readership genuinely faces. Order
is flexible. Each supplies several issues.

**Public-company register.** Hedging forward-looking statements. The polite
non-answer. Why "we will definitely ship in Q4" became a liability. Saying
nothing at length, and doing it gracefully.

**Integration and acquisition conversations.** Saying no to a team you just
acquired. Giving direction without sounding like an occupier. Asking for
information without implying an audit.

**English between non-native speakers.** The core insight most material misses:
these readers speak English mostly to other non-native speakers. An Italian
hedge lands as refusal to an American and as agreement to a Spaniard. Directness
calibration across L1s.

**Deferral and its costs.** Circle back, park it, take it offline, revisit. When
these read as organized and when they read as evasive.

**Disagreement in a flat culture.** Structures for pushing back upward where the
stated culture says hierarchy does not exist but the room says otherwise.

**Written tone without a voice.** Updates, docs, and messages that must carry
register with no prosody available to help.

**Announcing the unfinished.** Confidence about work in progress without
overclaiming.

**Meeting mechanics.** Interrupting, reclaiming the floor, closing a topic,
buying three seconds without filler.

**Gen-Z and informal register.** Terms in current use, taught as register
decisions rather than vocabulary.

## 7. Production workflow

1. Claude drafts the issue against this standard.
2. Netto reviews and approves the text before anything is built or scheduled.
   No issue ships unreviewed.
3. Any semantic or factual inconsistency is flagged for Netto's decision, never
   fixed unilaterally.
4. **The proofread skill is run on every issue, without exception**, before the
   text is placed into HTML. Structural and factual pass, deterministic
   house-style checks, house-style enforcement.
5. Built into the locked HTML template. Technical checks per EMAIL_STANDARD.md
   section 10.
6. Second proofread pass on the built HTML, since placing text into markup is
   itself a step where errors enter.
7. Test send to Netto. Opened on desktop and phone, and once with images
   blocked.
8. Scheduled in MailerLite.

### Protected content, critical

This newsletter deliberately contains incorrect English. The L1 trap in The
Gauge shows a learner error so the reader recognizes their own. Section 3 of
this document requires it.

A proofreading pass that silently corrects those errors destroys the teaching
point and ships an issue that contradicts itself. Every deliberate error must be
marked as protected before the pass runs, and confirmed intact afterward.

The same protection applies to:

- Learner language quoted as an example of what not to say
- Informal and Gen-Z terms, which are not misspellings
- Register-marked forms that a general style checker will flag as too casual
- British English quoted as contrast against the American default

If a proofreading pass returns an issue with no flagged items, treat that as
suspicious rather than clean. These issues always contain intentional errors.

## 8. Send schedule, locked

**Tuesday, 08:00, Europe/Rome. Never moved.**

Tuesday because Monday's triage is finished and the week is underway, while four
working days remain for the reader to attempt the One Rep in a real meeting.
Monday at 09:00 loses to weekend backlog. Friday undermines One Rep, since the
instruction lands as the week closes.

08:00 rather than 09:00 puts the letter at the top of a quiet inbox before the
first calls, and away from the hour when most scheduled sends arrive.

Consistency outranks optimization. A slot that moves never becomes a habit.

**Setup requirement:** the MailerLite account time zone must be set to
Europe/Rome. It defaults to UTC, which would deliver at 10:00 Italian time in
summer.

### Sending model

The Reading is **scheduled, not automated**. MailerLite automations are
trigger-based and suited to evergreen sequences. Each issue is topical and, per
section 7, cannot ship without review. Each issue is built, approved, and
scheduled individually into the fixed slot.

One genuine automation is worth building once six to eight issues exist: a
welcome sequence firing when a student joins the group, delivering the strongest
evergreen issues in order, so a student who starts in March is not dropped into
week 31 without context.

## 9. Visual template, locked

Approved August 12, 2026. Concept 2A, "The Editorial". Reference build:
`the-reading-01.html`. Structure is fixed; only content changes between issues.

### Order

1. **Utility bar**, near-black. Domain left, `WEEKLY · ISSUE NN · 3 MIN` right.
2. **Masthead**, white. Gauge mark at 76px (light-background variant), CONCEPT
   at 27px red with 9px tracking, MIND YOUR FUTURE beneath in gray.
3. **Photograph**, full width, black and white, no border.
4. **Hero block**, near-black. Dateline in tracked caps with CONCEPT and
   THE READING in red and the date in gray; headline in white uppercase sans at
   34px; standfirst in italic Georgia, gray.
5. **Five numbered sections** on white. Georgia numeral in red, tracked label,
   hairline rule to the right edge.
6. **Sign-off**, then LinkedIn tile at 18px in brand blue with the label in red.
7. **Footer**, near-black.

### The house device

Tinted ground `#F7F7F8` with a 3px red left rule. Applied to the dialogue, the
NOT THIS / SAY THIS pair, One Rep, and The Kit. Nothing else uses it.

### Type

Calibri, Carlito, Arial for body and labels. Georgia for quoted language,
standfirst, and section numerals. Note that Calibri is absent on macOS, so most
readers see Arial. The design must not depend on Calibri.

### Rhythm

Dark, white, photograph, dark, white, dark. The alternation is the structure.
Do not add further dark blocks.

### Photography

Every issue needs one: black and white, 1200px wide, JPEG at quality 80, under
about 80KB, filename `reading-NN-hero.jpg`. Must be owned or properly licensed.

**Known cost:** at 440px tall the photograph fills most of a phone screen, so
the headline sits below the fold. This is deliberate, the trade a magazine cover
makes. And readers with images blocked lose the top third entirely, though
everything from the dateline down still reads.

### Asset order, mandatory

Upload images to the repo root **before** loading the HTML into MailerLite.
Verify each returns 200 from outside. The editor preview and Apple Mail both
block remote images, so neither can confirm anything.

## 10. The pointer, locked

Every issue closes with one quiet line pointing to the book that takes the
week's topic further, placed after section 05 and before the sign-off, separated
by a hairline rule.

**Four books are available to point to:**

| Book | Points here when the issue covers |
| :---- | :---- |
| Professional English Practice C1 | Structures: hedging, emphasis, concession, timing |
| Professional English Practice C2 | Register shifts, implication, saying no without closing a door |
| It's Not Only a Phrasal Verb List | Particle-carried meaning, the deferral and process verbs |
| Calibrated Speech | Anything where the failure is sound rather than structure: stress placement, intonation, being understood |

**Rules:**

- **One pointer per issue.** Never two, even when two books genuinely fit. Two
  turns a close into a menu.
- **Omit it entirely** when no book fits well. A forced pointer is worse than
  none, and readers notice the weeks it is doing real work.
- **One sentence, no sales register.** State what the book covers, then the
  link. Never "buy", never "don't miss", never a button.
- **Set in body gray with the link in red**, smaller than the body text. It is
  a footnote, not a call to action.
- **UTM tagged:** `utm_medium=reading`, `utm_campaign=issueNN`,
  `utm_content=pointer`, so pointer traffic is measurable separately.
- **Name the unit number** where one applies, once unit-level anchors exist. The
  companion pages currently have no deep links, so issue 01 points at the book.

Issue 01 points to C1, since the teaching is hedging. The Kit is phrasal-verb
heavy and would also justify the phrasal verbs book, which is exactly the case
the one-pointer rule exists to settle.
