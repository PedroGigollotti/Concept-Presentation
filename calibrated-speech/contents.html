The pronunciation companion to Professional English Practice. Forty-four units on the sounds, stress, rhythm, and intonation of clear American English, every example spoken and ready to record against.

This is Calibrated Speech, the pronunciation companion to Professional English Practice. The main series trains what you say. This one trains how you sound while you say it, because a correct sentence still costs you the room when the listener has to work to decode it.

Forty-four units take you from the individual consonants and vowels, through word stress and the music of connected speech, to the intonation and delivery that carry a meeting. Each unit stands on its own, so you can work the book start to finish or open straight to the sound that is getting in your way this week.

Each unit follows the same cycle, so the shape of the book never surprises you once you know it.

The symbols. A short strip of the sounds the unit teaches, each one clickable, so you hear it before you read a word about it.

Why it matters. What this sound does to a listener, and what it costs you when it slips.

What your language does. The specific interference that pulls you off target.

How to make it. The physical instruction for mouth, breath, and voice, with a diagram of what your tongue is doing.

Practice and a game. Word banks, minimal pairs, an ear-training game, and full sentences, every item spoken by a calibrated American voice at natural and slow speed.

Record and compare. At the foot of every unit you record yourself and see your own voice drawn in red over the model in gray. You are comparing shapes, not chasing a score. This is where a sound you can hear becomes a sound you can make.

Two ways to work through it. Start to finish, for a full path from the sounds up to intonation. Or unit by unit, opening directly to whatever is holding you back right now.

Do not skip the record and compare. Reading about a sound changes nothing. Saying it, hearing it back, and adjusting is the whole method.

I started The Concept Method because I kept meeting brilliant professionals whose English was already good, and who were still not being heard. They had the words. What slipped was the sound: a vowel that turned one word into another, a stress that landed on the wrong syllable, a flat line where the meaning needed a lift. The ideas were right. The delivery was quietly costing them the room.

That gap is not a talent problem, and it is not about losing your accent. Your accent is yours to keep. This is about being understood the first time, without the listener working for it, and about carrying the small signals of stress, rhythm, and melody that tell people you mean exactly what you say.

So that is what this book teaches. Not a performance, and not an American mask, but control: the handful of sounds that actually change whether you are understood, and the music underneath your sentences that decides whether they land. Every clip here is calibrated, so the target you copy is the right one, every time.

Mind your future. It is closer than you think, and it is going to need people to hear you clearly the first time you speak.

Pedro Gigollotti NettoFounder, The Concept Method
